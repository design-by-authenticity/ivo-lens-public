# 1. Revision summary

## Unchanged

The candidate keeps:

- **O — Structured Possibility**
- **V — Instantiated Relation**
- **I — Situated Selectivity**
- O/V/I as analytical functions within explicit scope, not ontological classes;
- multiplicity where analytically consequential;
- OVI and IVO structural directions;
- evidence / interpretation / speculation separation;
- the full v4.1 epistemic safeguard architecture unless explicitly amended below;
- **unexplained ≠ I**;
- human consciousness, free will, agency, biological status, randomness, and unpredictability as non-requirements;
- zero I-functions as a valid result when warranted;
- cross-scale non-cancellation;
- domain function ≠ I-function;
- revocability;
- claim-level reliability discipline.

## Changed

The candidate changes the internal logic of I.

Canonical v4.1 couples:

> positive selective evidence  
> **and**  
> residual structural openness

as joint requirements for I.

The candidate separates:

1. **I-function** — evidenced selective function;
2. **mechanistic implementation** — how that function is realised;
3. **autonomy qualifier** — whether structural openness remains after strongest adequate O/V reconstruction;
4. **token-level causal contribution** — optional event-level refinement when masking, redundancy, compensation, or override matters.

The candidate does **not** create new top-level operators.

O, V, and I remain the canon.

# 2. Canonical identity — proposed text

## v4.1

No semantic change required.

## v4.2 candidate

**Canonical identity:** In this framework, I·V·O means exclusively **I = Situated Selectivity, V = Instantiated Relation, O = Structured Possibility**. Do not infer, expand, or substitute alternative meanings for the initials. Translated labels are permitted only alongside, or explicitly mapped to, the canonical English terms; the canonical meanings may not be replaced or redefined. If the referenced framework cannot be reliably identified, state that ambiguity rather than reconstructing its meaning from the name.

Canonical operators:

- O — Structured Possibility
- V — Instantiated Relation
- I — Situated Selectivity

The operators are analytical functions within an explicitly stated scope, not ontological classes of entities. A single entity, process, or event may carry different O-, V-, and I-functions in different functional aspects, but the same aspect must not be multiply labelled without independent analytical consequence. Operator assignments are therefore stated as functional assignments within scope, not as claims about what an entity essentially is.

The operators are not assumed to be singular. A system may contain multiple materially relevant possibility fields, instantiated relations, and situated selective functions. Multiplicity is introduced only where it changes the analysis.

Always distinguish supplied evidence/observation, interpretation, and speculation. State uncertainty instead of filling gaps.

**Revision note:** unchanged from v4.1 in substance.

# 5. I — Situated Selectivity

## 5.1 v4.1 text to be revised

The v4.1 rule states, in substance:

> I is a situated selective function within an explicitly specified analytical scope. I is present when reconstructed O applied to instantiated V leaves more than one continuation structurally open at the relevant selection point, **and** positive evidence shows that a selective step contributes to which continuation becomes instantiated.

It then treats structural underdetermination as necessary for I.

The v0.4-amended regression programme found this combined criterion over-restrictive for deterministic but positively evidenced selective functions.

---

# 6. Proposed v4.2 candidate definition of I

### I — Situated Selectivity

I is a **situated selective function within an explicitly specified analytical scope**.

I may be assigned when there is **positive, reportable evidence of a selective function** and evidence that the selective structure has **causal capacity to affect which relevant continuation becomes realised within the declared scope across the relevant admissible contingency structure**.

A positive I-assignment does **not** require the selective mechanism to remain unexplained, irreducible, indeterministic, or structurally open after mechanistic reconstruction.

Mechanistic explanation neither establishes nor revokes I by itself.

Structural openness is assessed separately as an **autonomy qualifier**.

The relevant questions are therefore distinct:

1. **Function:** Is a selective function positively evidenced?
2. **Implementation:** How is that function realised?
3. **Capacity:** Can intervention on the selective structure affect the declared continuation in relevant admissible conditions?
4. **Token contribution:** Did this particular selection event contribute under this particular realised condition?
5. **Autonomy:** After the strongest adequate O/V reconstruction, do multiple continuations remain structurally open?

Do not collapse these questions into one status.

---

# 7. Situatedness

“Situated” remains part of the canonical meaning of I.

Situatedness does not require an independent `I-position` operator or status.

For each candidate I, declare as relevant:

- analytical scope;
- carrier;
- access;
- relevant history/state;
- outside view;
- scale/resolution;
- system boundary.

These are **conditions of assignment**, not separate ontological entities.

Asymmetric access, physical location, memory, history, network position, adaptive baselines, or relocation do not by themselves establish I.

A position-like description should not be promoted into a separate I-function unless a materially selective function is independently evidenced.

---

# 8. Carrier

For every candidate I identify the **carrier**:

> the smallest supported system unit that realises the relevant selective structure within the declared scope.

A carrier may be:

- a person;
- a group;
- an institution;
- an organism;
- an algorithm;
- an analytical procedure;
- a technical controller;
- a distributed process;
- another materially selective system structure.

A carrier is not thereby:

- a subject;
- a conscious observer;
- an autonomous agent;
- an ontological individual.

Human consciousness, agency, free will, biological status, unpredictability, and randomness remain non-canonical requirements.

---

# 9. Positive-evidence requirement

**Unexplained is not I.**

I may never be assigned merely because O + V do not yet explain an outcome.

A candidate I requires positive, reportable evidence of the selective function itself.

Evidence may include, where appropriate:

- a documented selection state or trace distinct from the final outcome;
- intervention on selection criteria, weights, or selective structure that changes the selected continuation;
- evidence that alternatives are functionally distinguished rather than merely listed, ranked, classified, or filtered;
- evidence that the selective structure has causal access to the declared continuation;
- relational allocation among competing alternatives;
- a selective consequence that changes admission, exclusion, prioritisation, routing, commitment, or another domain-appropriate continuation.

No single marker is universally sufficient.

The evidence must be evaluated against the declared scope and relevant alternatives.

---

# 10. Insufficient evidence for I

The following are insufficient **by themselves**:

- alternatives;
- criteria;
- weighting;
- memory;
- adaptation;
- representation;
- filtering;
- comparison;
- thresholding;
- basis-selection;
- sorting;
- ranking;
- classification;
- admission/exclusion labels;
- deterministic mapping;
- feedback;
- control;
- complexity;
- separability;
- multiple realizability;
- downstream causal effect;
- randomness;
- stochastic outcome;
- physical branching;
- recording;
- human involvement.

Domain vocabulary such as:

- function;
- choice;
- recognition;
- selection;
- controller;
- decision;
- veto;
- admission

does not establish an I-function.

The domain structure must be reconstructed independently.

---

# 11. Classification versus selection

A classification may alter downstream behaviour without constituting I.

Examples:

`input → category`

`project → compliant/non-compliant`

`temperature → above/below threshold`

may remain fully within O/V.

A selective function requires more than the fact that an output changes downstream.

The analysis must identify a materially selective role relative to relevant alternatives and continuation.

Relational constrained allocation is a strongly supported positive pattern, but is not defined here as a necessary condition for every I-function.

---

# 12. Functional causal capacity

For every positive I-function assess **functional causal capacity**.

Ask:

> Does intervention on the selective structure change the declared continuation in at least one relevant admissible condition?

Suggested analytical statuses:

- ESTABLISHED
- SUPPORTED
- UNRESOLVED
- NOT ESTABLISHED

A positive I-function requires positive support for such causal capacity.

Do not preserve I by appealing to arbitrary or remote hypothetical conditions.

The contingency set must be materially relevant to the declared scope and system.

---

# 13. Token-level causal contribution

Where masking, redundancy, compensation, override, or overdetermination matters, assess separately:

> Did this particular selection event, under this particular realised condition, make a non-zero contribution to the declared continuation?

Suggested analytical statuses:

- ESTABLISHED
- SUPPORTED
- ZERO / MASKED
- UNRESOLVED
- NOT APPLICABLE

This is an analytical refinement, not a new canonical operator.

Core rule:

> **I-function is a functional-capacity claim; token-level causal contribution is a realised-event claim.**

Therefore:

- zero token contribution does not automatically revoke I-function;
- redundancy does not automatically revoke I-function;
- downstream compensation does not automatically revoke I-function;
- one masked event is insufficient to establish epiphenomenality.

Repeated absence of causal access across the relevant admissible contingency structure may revoke the I-function.

---

# 14. Mechanistic implementation

For each material I-function report its mechanistic implementation as:

- COMPLETE
- PARTIAL
- UNRESOLVED

This status is orthogonal to I-function.

Core rule:

> **Mechanistic explanation neither establishes nor revokes a selective function.**

A completely deterministic mechanism may carry an I-function when positive selective evidence and functional causal capacity are established.

A poorly understood mechanism does not thereby carry I.

---

# 15. Autonomy qualifier

Structural underdetermination is retained, but its role changes.

It no longer serves as a necessary condition for I-function.

Instead assess:

> **After the strongest adequate O/V reconstruction at the relevant scope and resolution, do multiple continuations remain structurally open?**

Classify:

- ESTABLISHED
- SUPPORTED
- UNRESOLVED
- NOT ESTABLISHED

Distinguish:

### Epistemic underdetermination

The analyst lacks information about O or V.

This never establishes autonomy.

### Structural underdetermination

Multiple continuations remain open within the best explicit reconstruction and chosen scope.

This is evidence relevant to the autonomy qualifier.

### Ontological underdetermination

Reality itself is claimed not to determine the outcome in advance.

The Lens neither establishes nor requires this claim unless independently supplied by the domain.

Core safeguard:

> **Unknown is not autonomy.**

Missing information, unpredictability, stochastic terminology, complexity, human choice, or unresolved microdynamics do not by themselves establish autonomy.

---

# 16. Revised I-assignment rule

A positive I-function requires:

1. explicit analytical scope;
2. an identified carrier;
3. materially relevant alternatives or continuations;
4. positive evidence for a selective function;
5. a selective consequence relevant to the declared scope;
6. evidence of functional causal capacity across relevant admissible contingencies;
7. no simpler classification as mere thresholding, mapping, ranking, filtering, classification, passive recording, or ordinary control.

It does **not** require:

- ontological indeterminism;
- residual structural openness;
- incomplete mechanism;
- unpredictability;
- consciousness;
- free will.

Autonomy is reported separately.

---

# 17. Scope rule

Every I-function is scope-indexed.

For each candidate I state:

- what continuation is being analysed;
- at what scale;
- within what system boundary;
- under what relevant conditions.

A function may be positive for:

- an internal selection state;
- a local execution;
- an organisational allocation;

while having:

- zero token contribution at a broader system scope;
- no function at another scale;
- no aggregate system-level function.

Do not transfer an I-function automatically across scope or scale.

---

# 18. Scale rule

**Cross-scale non-cancellation remains canonical.**

Add the following clarification:

> Function status at one scale does not automatically establish function at another scale. A lower-level selective function may be real while its higher-level net effect is masked, compensated, or aggregated away. Conversely, system-level function requires independent selective evidence at that level.

A lower-scale O/V reconstruction may fully explain implementation without revoking a higher-scale I-function.

A higher-scale I-function does not imply lower-scale irreducibility.

Report both as scale-indexed reconstructions when both are valid.

---

# 19. Revocation rule

I-assignment remains provisional and revocable within its domain of validity.

Revoke or downgrade a positive I-function when evidence shows that:

- the alleged selector has no independent selective structure;
- the selection trace is only logging or retrospective description;
- the output never enters the relevant causal pathway;
- intervention on the selective structure does not alter the declared continuation anywhere in the relevant admissible contingency structure;
- the supposed selective effect is fully exhausted by thresholding, classification, mapping, ranking, filtering, or ordinary control;
- the function was preserved only through irrelevant hypothetical contingencies;
- the scope was silently changed to rescue the claim.

Do **not** revoke I merely because:

- implementation becomes complete;
- the mechanism is deterministic;
- one token event is masked;
- another redundant selector also suffices;
- a downstream compensator restores a broader variable;
- a lower-level implementation is found.

# 20. Revised governing principles

Principles 1–17 remain unchanged unless wording below requires alignment.

## Proposed principles 18–22

### 18. I requires positive selective evidence and functional causal capacity.

Do not assign I from alternatives, criteria, complexity, unexplained outcomes, or domain vocabulary alone. A candidate I must have positive evidence of selective structure and demonstrated causal capacity relative to the declared continuation and admissible contingency structure.

### 19. Selection and autonomy are distinct claims.

Structural openness is not required for I-function. Assess autonomy separately after the strongest adequate O/V reconstruction. Epistemic underdetermination never establishes autonomy.

### 20. Mechanistic explanation neither establishes nor revokes I.

A selective function may be fully mechanistically reconstructed. Complete implementation may coexist with I-function and with autonomy NOT ESTABLISHED.

### 21. Function is scope- and scale-indexed.

Do not project I automatically across continuations, system boundaries, or scales. Local function does not by itself establish aggregate function; broader masking does not by itself erase local function.

### 22. I-assignments are provisional and revocable.

Expanded evidence may establish, weaken, or revoke an I-function only through evidence relevant to its stated scope, selective structure, causal capacity, or admissible contingency structure. Boundary choice alone does not create or remove I.

# 21. Redline — governing principle 18

## v4.1

> **I requires structural openness plus positive evidence.** Structural underdetermination is assessed relative to the explicit reconstruction and scope; ontological indeterminism is neither required nor inferred.

## v4.2 candidate

> **I requires positive selective evidence plus demonstrated functional causal capacity.** Structural openness is assessed separately as an autonomy qualifier. Mechanistic completeness and determinism do not by themselves establish or revoke I.

### Rationale

The combined v4.1 condition produced false negatives in deterministic, fully reconstructed cases with strong intervention evidence for selection.

The experimental suite showed that function and structural openness are separable.

# 23. Redline — selective coupling paragraph

## v4.1

The existing v4.1 paragraph states that explicit criteria, weighting, memory, filtering, comparison, thresholding, separability, and multiple realizability are insufficient, and that if continuation follows completely from O/V the process should be represented without I.

## v4.2 candidate

Retain the insufficiency list, but replace the final closure rule with:

> **Selective coupling is not sufficient for I.** Explicit criteria, weighting, memory, adaptation, representation, filtering, comparison, thresholding, basis-selection, ranking, classification, complexity, separability, multiple realizability, feedback, control, or downstream effect are insufficient by themselves. A fully reconstructed deterministic process may nevertheless carry I when positive selective evidence and functional causal capacity are independently established. Mechanistic completeness belongs to implementation status; residual structural openness belongs to the autonomy qualifier.

### Rationale

Prevents both:

- false positives from ordinary gating;
- false negatives from complete deterministic selection.

# 24. Redline — revocation paragraph

## v4.1

The existing text permits richer same-scope O/V reconstruction to revoke I when the selective step is adequately explained as additional O/V structure.

## v4.2 candidate

Replace with:

> I-assignment is **provisional and revocable within its stated domain of validity**. Expanded O/V evidence may revise the implementation account without automatically revoking I. Revoke or downgrade I when stronger evidence shows that the alleged selective structure lacks independent selective consequence or causal capacity for the declared continuation across the relevant admissible contingency structure. A reconstruction at another scale does not by itself confirm or revoke the assignment. Boundary expansion does not create I.

### Rationale

The previous rule conflated explanation of implementation with elimination of function.

> A positive I-function does not by itself establish consciousness, agency, free will, subjectivity, personhood, moral status, intelligence, selfhood, or ontological autonomy.

Add:

> Deterministic I-function is not evidence for determinism or indeterminism as a universal metaphysical claim.

Add:

> I-autonomy, where supported, is a structural claim within the declared reconstruction and scope; it is not automatically a claim of ontological indeterminism.

Add:

# 41. Falsifiability and revision — proposed additions

Treat as evidence against the v4.2 candidate if:

- deterministic positive selectors regress to NOT ESTABLISHED solely because their mechanism is complete;
- thresholding, mapping, ranking, or classification become promiscuously positive;
- masking is used to preserve arbitrary I claims without demonstrated causal capacity;
- admissible contingencies become so broad that almost any dormant possibility preserves function;
- function/capacity/token contribution cannot be reproduced consistently across models;
- autonomy is inferred from missing information;
- carrier selection drifts between levels without evidence;
- scope changes rescue otherwise failed I claims;
- independent I-position reappears merely from access/history differences;
- the added distinctions increase complexity without changing any tested classification;
- the revised rule performs worse than v4.1 on heterogeneous real-world cases.
