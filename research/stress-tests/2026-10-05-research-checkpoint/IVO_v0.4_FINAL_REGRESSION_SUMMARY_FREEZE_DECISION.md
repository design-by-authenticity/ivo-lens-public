# 1. Purpose of this document

This document closes the v0.4 experimental regression phase.

The purpose of v0.4 was to determine whether the experimental I-semantics could:

- preserve genuine positive selective functions;
- prevent inflation from thresholding, mapping, ranking, filtering, classification, randomness, and ordinary control;
- separate function from mechanistic implementation;
- separate functional capacity from token-level causal contribution;
- retain scope and scale discipline;
- test whether I-position deserves an independent analytical status;
- preserve the epistemic safeguards of canonical v4.1.

The final full regression matrix reports:

> **PASS WITH QUALIFICATION**

No unexpected classification drift was found after the T3B, T4, and T5 corrections.

This document therefore freezes the current experimental architecture.

---

# 2. Freeze decision

## Decision

**FREEZE v0.4-amended as the current stable experimental reference.**

This freeze means:

- semantic rules are not to be changed during comparison or replication;
- existing regression cases are preserved unchanged;
- future changes require a new version;
- canonical v4.1 remains unchanged;
- v0.4-amended is not yet canonical.

The freeze is justified because:

1. near-miss negatives remain negative;
2. positive regression cases remain positive;
3. the T-series remains internally coherent after amendment;
4. no regression-breaking inconsistency is exposed;
5. open boundaries are now explicit rather than silently patched.

# 5. T-series verdict

## T1 — Partial causation

**PASS**

A selective function may be positive without being the sole cause of the realised continuation.

Non-zero interventionally supported causal capacity is sufficient when independent selective evidence is present.

---

## T2 — Redundant selectors

**PASS**

Redundancy does not revoke function.

A selector may retain I-function even when its token-level effect is masked by another selector.

Single-intervention invariance is insufficient to establish irrelevance.

Relevant contingencies and joint interventions may be required.

---

## T3 — Nested / multi-stage selection

**PASS WITH T3B CORRECTION**

The following distinction remains stable:

- ranking alone → NOT ESTABLISHED;
- shortlist / relational allocation → ESTABLISHED;
- fixed compliance gate → NOT ESTABLISHED;
- final selection → ESTABLISHED;
- execution alone → NOT ESTABLISHED.

Nested selection can contain multiple functionally distinct stages without carrier collapse.

---

## T3B — Veto versus threshold

**PASS WITH SEMANTIC QUALIFICATION**

T3B established:

- downstream causal consequence alone is insufficient;
- admission/exclusion alone is insufficient;
- fixed classification remains within O/V;
- relational constrained allocation is strong positive evidence for I-function.

However:

> relational constrained allocation is supported as a sufficient discriminator, not as a necessary condition for every possible I-function.

This remains an open semantic boundary.

---

## T4 — I-position boundary

**PASS AS A NEGATIVE ARCHITECTURAL RESULT**

T4 did not establish an independent positive I-position.

Full O/V reconstruction preserved all tested position-like distinctions when it included:

- history;
- memory;
- stored state;
- baselines;
- relocation history;
- update rules;
- connectivity;
- access structure.

Therefore:

> independent I-position is not retained as an active experimental dimension.

Instead, situated analysis remains mandatory through:

- scope;
- carrier;
- access;
- history/state;
- outside view.

The I-position hypothesis remains available only as an unresolved research hypothesis.

---

## T5 — Scale interaction

**PASS AFTER SEMANTIC CORRECTION**

T5 established that:

- local function does not automatically project to system-level function;
- scale and scope must be analysed separately;
- full compensation can mask a token-level effect without erasing functional capacity;
- partial compensation becomes a T1-type partial-causation case;
- deterministic feedback control does not automatically become I-function.

T5 required the final major semantic correction:

> separate I-function / functional causal capacity from token-level causal contribution.

## 6.2 I-function

An I-function is:

> **a positively evidenced selective function with demonstrated causal capacity to affect which continuation becomes realised within an explicitly declared analytical scope across the relevant admissible contingency structure.**

Allowed statuses:

- ESTABLISHED
- SUPPORTED
- UNRESOLVED
- NOT ESTABLISHED

---

## 6.3 Functional causal capacity

Functional causal capacity asks:

> Can intervention on the selective structure affect the declared continuation in at least one relevant admissible condition?

Allowed statuses:

- ESTABLISHED
- SUPPORTED
- UNRESOLVED
- NOT ESTABLISHED

Functional capacity is not identical to realised event contribution.

---

## 6.4 Token-level causal contribution

Token-level causal contribution asks:

> Did this particular selection event, under this particular realised condition, make a non-zero contribution to the declared continuation?

Allowed statuses:

- ESTABLISHED

- ZERO / MASKED
- UNRESOLVED
- NOT APPLICABLE

A zero or masked token-level contribution does not automatically revoke I-function.

# 8. Stable positive evidence pattern

The strongest tested positive I-function pattern is:

- multiple relevant alternatives;
- positive selective structure;
- independently evidenced selective state/event;
- selective consequence;
- demonstrated causal capacity across relevant admissible contingencies;
- explicit analytical scope.

Strong positive evidence may include:

- criterion-sensitive selection;
- pre-execution selection state;
- intervention-sensitive selection;
- relational allocation;
- continuation-changing selective capacity.

Relational constrained allocation is currently the strongest positive selector paradigm, but is not frozen as a necessary universal condition.

---

# 9. Scope rule

All I-function claims are scope-specific.

A function may be:

- positive for an internal selection scope;
- positive for a local execution scope;
- masked for a broader system scope;
- irrelevant to another continuation.

No function status may be projected automatically across scopes.

A failed claim may not be rescued by silently changing scope.

---

# 10. Scale rule

Function does not automatically inherit across scale.

A local I-function may remain real while:

- its higher-level net effect is zero;
- a downstream compensator cancels it;
- the system-level continuation remains invariant.

Likewise:

> local positive function does not imply a new aggregate system-level I-function.

System-level positive status requires independent selective evidence at that level.

---

# 11. Capacity versus contribution rule

This is the final major correction of v0.4.

> **Function belongs to a selective architecture relative to a declared scope. Contribution belongs to a particular realised event under a particular condition.**

Therefore:

- masking can suppress token-level contribution without suppressing function;
- redundancy can suppress token-level necessity without suppressing function;
- compensation can suppress net effect without suppressing demonstrated functional capacity;
- repeated absence of effect across the full admissible contingency structure may revoke function.

---

# 12. O/V preservation rule

O and V remain fully available for mechanistic reconstruction.

The protocol must not create I by artificially removing:

- thresholds;
- mappings;
- histories;
- internal states;
- couplings;
- causal relations;
- control loops;
- selection events.

Selection events may be represented in V.

I-function classifies their functional selective role.

This is not a competition between V and I.

---

# 13. Final position decision

The v0.3/v0.4 attempt to establish an independent I-position dimension did not survive full O/V exhaustion.

Therefore:

**Independent I-position: NOT ACTIVE**

Retain only:

> POSITION HYPOTHESIS — UNRESOLVED

for future dedicated research.

A future recovery of independent I-position requires a case where:

1. a stable positional distinction exists;
2. full O/V reconstruction is maximally specified;
3. removing position still loses an operationally identifiable analytical distinction;
4. the result does not inflate sensors, buffers, channels, network nodes, memory, or context-dependent mappings;
5. the finding replicates across structurally different cases.

Until then:

> situatedness remains mandatory without independent operator status.

# 15. Open boundaries at freeze

The freeze is qualified by four explicit open issues.

## 15.1 Necessary condition for I-function

Relational constrained allocation is sufficient positive evidence.

It is not proven necessary.

Future work may discover other structurally valid selective functions.

---

## 15.2 Autonomy doctrine

The I-autonomy branch is less mature than the I-function branch.

NM-04 supports autonomy within an explicitly stochastic model.

NM-05 remains unresolved under incomplete microdynamics.

Further work is permitted but not required before freezing v0.4.

---

## 15.3 Admissible-contingency boundary

Functional capacity depends on:

> relevant admissible contingencies.

The protocol must avoid both:

- too narrow a contingency space that suppresses real function;
- too broad a hypothetical space that preserves function artificially.

This remains a governance and operationalisation issue.

---

## 15.4 Position hypothesis

Independent I-position remains unresolved.

No current positive case survives full O/V exhaustion.

This is not a blocker for v0.4-amended because the active architecture no longer depends on positive I-position.
