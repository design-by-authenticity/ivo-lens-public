# 1. Purpose

This document compares the frozen canonical I·V·O Lens v4.1 with the frozen experimental v0.4-amended semantics.

The review has five purposes:

1. identify exactly where v0.4-amended agrees with v4.1;
2. identify where it materially departs from v4.1;
3. determine which differences are clarifications, which are extensions, and which are genuine semantic revisions;
4. assess backward compatibility and claim-surface risk;
5. determine whether the evidence currently justifies a canonical revision proposal.

This document does **not** itself revise the canon.

Its role is governance:

> determine whether a canon change should now be drafted, deferred, limited, or rejected.

# 2. Canonical baseline: v4.1

Canonical v4.1 defines:

- **O — Structured Possibility**
- **V — Instantiated Relation**
- **I — Situated Selectivity**

The key v4.1 safeguard for I is that I is not established merely because:

- something is unexplained;
- multiple outcomes are possible;
- information is processed;
- criteria exist;
- weighting exists;
- memory exists;
- filtering occurs;
- thresholds are applied;
- a system is complex;
- a mechanism is incomplete.

The core v4.1 rule can be summarised as:

> I requires structural openness plus positive evidence of a selective step contributing to which continuation becomes instantiated.

The canon also protects:

> **unexplained ≠ I**

and requires that epistemic underdetermination not be mistaken for structural underdetermination.

These safeguards remain a major strength of v4.1.

## I-autonomy

Residual structural openness after the strongest adequate O/V reconstruction.

# 4. Executive comparison

The strongest difference between v4.1 and v0.4-amended is not a change to O or V.

It is a decomposition of the claims previously bundled inside I.

v4.1 effectively asks one strong combined question:

> Is there positive selectivity **and** does structural openness remain?

v0.4-amended separates that into:

1. **Is there a selective function?**
2. **How is it implemented?**
3. **Does structural openness remain?**
4. **Did this particular event contribute under this particular condition?**

This separation is the central candidate canon revision.

# 6. Primary semantic conflict

The major conflict is the relation between:

- positive selection evidence;
- mechanistic completeness;
- structural openness.

## v4.1 logic

A positive I-claim requires:

positive selective evidence  
+

structural openness after O/V reconstruction.

This creates a potential failure pattern:

better mechanistic evidence  
→ stronger O/V reconstruction  
→ less structural openness  
→ I disappears.

The regression programme produced cases where this happened despite strong evidence for a real selective function.

---

# 7. Positive-I false negative under v4.1

The strongest experimental cases were:

- deterministic algorithm;
- autonomous energy controller;
- formal organisational project selection.

In each case:

- multiple relevant alternatives existed;
- selection was explicitly evidenced;
- intervention on selection criteria altered the selected continuation;
- the selection state causally affected execution;
- the mechanism was completely reconstructable.

Under the v4.1 combined criterion:

complete reconstruction closes structural openness.

Therefore positive I risks disappearing.

Under v0.4-amended:

- **I-function remains ESTABLISHED**
- **mechanistic implementation = COMPLETE**
- **I-autonomy = NOT ESTABLISHED**

This is the central empirical argument for revision.

---

# 8. Core revision candidate

The strongest supported experimental principle is:

> **A selective function does not cease to be a function merely because its mechanism is well understood.**

This implies:

> mechanistic reducibility and functional selectivity are not the same claim.

Therefore structural underdetermination should not be required for the existence of a selective function.

Instead:

> structural underdetermination belongs to the stronger autonomy claim.

This is the single most important candidate change to canonical v4.1.

---

# 9. Proposed semantic separation

The experimental evidence supports separating:

## A. Selective function

Question:

> Is there positive evidence for a selective architecture with causal capacity to affect which relevant continuation becomes realised?

## B. Implementation

Question:

> How is that selective function realised?

## C. Autonomy

Question:

> After the strongest adequate O/V reconstruction, do multiple continuations remain structurally open?

These are empirically separable.

The regression suite contains:

- function + complete mechanism + no autonomy;
- function + partial mechanism + unresolved autonomy;
- no function + autonomy supported;
- no function + autonomy unresolved.

Therefore the dimensions are not interchangeable.

---

# 10. v4.1 versus v0.4-amended: I-function

## v4.1

Selectivity is coupled to structural openness.

## v0.4-amended

Selectivity is established by positive functional evidence.

The minimum experimental requirement is not merely:

- alternatives;
- threshold;
- ranking;
- comparison;
- downstream effect.

Rather, the strongest stable pattern is:

- relevant alternatives;
- independent selective structure;
- selective consequence;
- demonstrated functional causal capacity;
- explicit scope.

This retains anti-inflation discipline.

---

# 11. Causal capacity versus token-level contribution

This distinction does not exist explicitly in canonical v4.1.

The regression programme shows that it matters.

## Functional causal capacity

A selector may be genuinely capable of affecting a continuation across relevant admissible contingencies.

## Token-level contribution

A specific selection event may contribute:

- positively;
- partially;
- or not at all because it is masked.

Examples:

- redundant selectors;
- downstream compensation;
- veto or override conditions.

Therefore:

> zero token-level contribution does not automatically imply absence of I-function.

This is an experimental extension, but it follows directly from the need to prevent false negatives under redundancy and masking.

---

# 12. Thresholding, mapping, classification and ranking

v0.4-amended strengthens the anti-inflation boundaries of v4.1.

The regression programme preserves:

- filtering ≠ I-function;
- thresholding ≠ I-function;
- fixed mapping ≠ I-function;
- classification ≠ I-function;
- ranking ≠ I-function;
- recording ≠ I-function;
- random outcome ≠ I-function;
- physical branching ≠ I-function;
- deterministic feedback ≠ I-function.

This is important for governance.

The proposed revision does not simply make I broader.

It makes the positive and negative criteria more discriminating.

---

# 13. Relational allocation

T3B established a strong positive discriminator:

> relational constrained allocation among multiple relevant alternatives can provide sufficient positive evidence for I-function.

Examples include:

- top-k selection;
- competitive allocation;
- constrained admission.

But the regression programme did **not** establish:

> relational constrained allocation is necessary for all I-function.

Therefore this criterion must not be canonised as the universal essence of I.

Governance status:

**SUPPORTED SUFFICIENT PATTERN — NOT NECESSARY DEFINITION**

---

# 14. I-position comparison

The experimental programme initially attempted to decompose “Situated” into an independent I-position dimension.

That attempt did not survive regression.

T4 showed that:

- asymmetric access;
- memory;
- history;
- context;
- adaptive baselines;
- relocation persistence

remain fully representable in O/V when O/V is allowed to include historical state, storage, update rules, and connectivity.

Therefore:

> no independent positive I-position dimension is currently justified.

This does **not** remove situatedness.

Instead, situatedness becomes mandatory analytical structure through:

- scope;
- carrier;
- access;
- history/state;
- outside view.

---

# 15. Governance consequence for “Situated”

Canonical v4.1 names I:

> **Situated Selectivity**

The experimental evidence does not justify changing that name.

It does suggest changing how “Situated” is operationalised.

Rather than requiring an independent positional sub-operator:

> situatedness should constrain how a selective claim is scoped, carried, and interpreted.

Thus:

- where is the function instantiated?
- what carrier realises it?
- what can that carrier access?
- what is outside view?
- at what scale is the claim made?

This is compatible with the canonical wording while avoiding an unsupported I-position ontology.

---

# 16. I-autonomy comparison

v4.1 currently embeds structural openness into the positive I-condition.

v0.4-amended separates it as I-autonomy.

The autonomy doctrine remains conservative.

I-autonomy is not established by:

- uncertainty;
- unknown microstate;
- unpredictability;
- complexity;
- human choice;
- stochastic language.

The experimental evidence supports:

- NM-04: autonomy may be SUPPORTED within an explicitly stochastic model;
- NM-05: autonomy remains UNRESOLVED if unknown microconditions might close the branch;
- deterministic complete systems: autonomy NOT ESTABLISHED.

This branch is coherent but less mature than I-function.

Governance implication:

> autonomy can be separated conceptually before it is promoted to a fully canonical sub-status system.

# 18. Claim-surface analysis

A canon change could increase the number of systems eligible for positive I-function.

This creates a publication-surface risk.

Without safeguards, readers might infer that:

- algorithms are conscious;
- deterministic controllers possess agency;
- every decision rule is I;
- every feedback loop is selective.

None of these follows.

Therefore any canon revision must explicitly state:

> I-function is an analytical functional classification, not an attribution of consciousness, agency, subjectivity, personhood, or ontological autonomy.

This safeguard is essential.

---

# 19. Human-privilege safeguard

The revised semantics must remain carrier-neutral.

The same structure must receive the same classification whether instantiated by:

- human;
- organisation;
- software;
- controller;
- biological subsystem;
- distributed procedure.

Human involvement alone cannot establish:

- I-function;
- autonomy;
- special positional status.

This principle has survived regression and should be retained.

---

# 20. Scope safeguard

A future canon revision should make scope explicit.

An I-function may be:

- positive for an internal decision;
- positive for local execution;
- masked at system scale;
- irrelevant to a different continuation.

Therefore:

> I-function status must always be indexed to a declared continuation or analytical scope.

This is stronger than current v4.1 practice and is supported by NM-08, T3, and T5.

---

# 21. Scale safeguard

Function does not automatically propagate across levels.

A local selector can be real without creating:

- a system-level selector;
- a group mind;
- an organisational agent;
- an autonomous higher-order I.

Therefore:

> scale transitions require fresh evidence.

This safeguard should accompany any canon revision.

---

# 22. O/V preservation safeguard

A revised canon must explicitly forbid using I to erase ordinary mechanism.

O/V must remain allowed to contain:

- selection events;
- histories;
- thresholds;
- comparisons;
- control loops;
- internal states;
- mappings;
- causal chains.

I-function identifies a functional role.

It does not compete with V for ownership of the event.

This is essential to prevent semantic double counting.

---

# 23. Strongest case for revision

The strongest argument for a canonical change is:

1. v4.1 correctly protects against unexplained → I;
2. but it couples positive function to structural openness;
3. deterministic but genuinely selective systems can therefore become false negatives;
4. v0.4-amended separates selection from autonomy;
5. regression testing shows this can be done without reopening the known near-miss false positives;
6. the full regression matrix remains stable after correction.

Therefore:

> the current canon appears over-restrictive in one specific respect: it requires autonomy-like openness for function-like selectivity.

---

# 24. Strongest case against immediate revision

The strongest reasons to defer immediate canon replacement are:

## A. The experimental architecture is recent

Internal consistency is demonstrated only against the current regression suite.

## B. Autonomy is less mature

The I-autonomy branch has fewer discriminating tests than I-function.

## C. Admissible contingency requires governance

The phrase:

> relevant admissible contingency structure

must be operationalised carefully to prevent arbitrary preservation of functions through remote hypotheticals.

## D. Cross-model reproducibility is still required

The semantics should be tested by independent model implementations without hidden coordination.

## E. External domain validation remains limited

Most regressions are deliberately constructed cases.

A canon revision should also survive heterogeneous real-world analyses.

---

# 25. Revision options

## Option A — No canon change

Keep v4.1 fully unchanged.

### Advantage

Maximum stability.

### Cost

The positive-I false negative remains.

Deterministic selectivity remains difficult to represent without forcing structural openness.

---

## Option B — Minimal canon patch

Retain the existing structure but change only the I rule:

> structural openness is no longer required for I-function, but is reported separately as an autonomy qualification.

### Advantage

Small publication surface.

High backward compatibility.

### Cost

May leave implementation, scope, capacity, and token-contribution distinctions under-specified.

---

## Option C — Moderate revision

Retain O/V/I canon but revise I into:

- situated scope/carrier constraints;
- I-function;
- autonomy qualifier;
- implementation status.

Functional capacity and token-level contribution appear as analytical reporting fields, not new operators.

### Advantage

Captures almost all validated experimental findings without proliferating canonical operators.

### Cost

Larger documentation change.

---

## Option D — Full structural rewrite

Make:

- I-function;
- I-autonomy;
- causal capacity;
- token contribution

fully canonical independent subdimensions.

### Advantage

Maximum explicitness.

### Cost

High complexity, high publication surface, greater risk of premature canonisation.

---

# 26. Recommended governance option

**Recommend Option C — Moderate revision proposal.**

Reason:

Option C preserves the canonical triad:

- O
- V
- I

while correcting the strongest validated weakness.

It does not turn every experimental reporting distinction into a new operator.

Under Option C:

## O

unchanged.

## V

unchanged.

## I

remains Situated Selectivity.

But canonical I is clarified into:

### Situated conditions

Mandatory:

- scope;
- carrier;
- access/outside view where relevant.

### I-function

Positive selective function based on demonstrated causal capacity.

### Autonomy qualifier

Report separately whether structural openness remains after strongest O/V reconstruction.

### Mechanistic implementation

Report separately as:

- complete;
- partial;
- unresolved.

### Token contribution

Use where causal masking/redundancy/compensation matters, but do not make it a core operator.

This is the smallest revision that captures the experimental gain.

# 27. Candidate revised I principle

A future canon proposal could test language close to:

> **I — Situated Selectivity**  
> I is established when positive evidence supports a selective function with demonstrated capacity to affect which relevant continuation becomes realised within the explicitly declared analytical scope. Mechanistic explanation neither establishes nor revokes I. Structural openness after the strongest adequate O/V reconstruction is a separate autonomy claim and must not be inferred from missing knowledge.

This is not yet adopted.

It is a candidate governance formulation.

---

# 28. Candidate anti-inflation clause

Any future revision should preserve an explicit negative clause:

> The existence of alternatives, information, criteria, ranking, filtering, thresholding, mapping, memory, control, stochasticity, or downstream effect is insufficient by itself to establish I.

And:

> Unknown, unexplained, unpredictable, or complex is not evidence of autonomy.

---

# 29. Candidate scope clause

A future canon proposal should add:

> Every positive I claim must declare the continuation and analytical scope to which the selective function is attributed.

And:

> A positive I claim at one scope does not automatically transfer to another scope or scale.

---

# 30. Candidate carrier clause

A future canon proposal should add:

> The carrier is the smallest supported system unit that realises the relevant selective structure. A carrier need not be a subject, agent, person, or ontological entity.

This preserves:

- technical systems;
- organisations;
- humans;
- distributed procedures

without anthropomorphic inflation.

---

# 31. Candidate implementation clause

A future canon proposal should add:

> Mechanistic implementation must be reported separately from I status.

Suggested statuses:

- COMPLETE
- PARTIAL
- UNRESOLVED

Core rule:

> complete implementation does not revoke an evidenced selective function.

---

# 32. Candidate autonomy clause

A future canon proposal could add:

> **Autonomy qualifier:** after the strongest adequate O/V reconstruction, determine whether multiple continuations remain structurally open at the relevant transition.

Suggested statuses:

- ESTABLISHED
- SUPPORTED
- UNRESOLVED
- NOT ESTABLISHED

Safeguard:

> epistemic underdetermination does not establish autonomy.

---

# 33. Candidate token-contribution clause

Token-level causal contribution should remain an analytical refinement rather than a canonical operator unless future testing shows otherwise.

Use it when:

- redundancy;
- compensation;
- overdetermination;
- masking;
- override

make event-level causation ambiguous.

Suggested statuses:

- ESTABLISHED
- SUPPORTED
- ZERO / MASKED
- UNRESOLVED
- NOT APPLICABLE

Before any canonical revision is adopted, require:

1. frozen v0.4-amended reference;
2. frozen v4.1 reference;
3. clause-by-clause semantic diff;
4. full regression suite unchanged;
5. cross-model reproducibility;
6. at least several heterogeneous real-world domain tests;
7. backward-compatibility review;
8. failure-mode review;
9. claim-surface / misuse review;
10. explicit governance approval;
11. versioned canonical candidate;
12. final freeze before publication.

# 35. Cross-model reproducibility requirement

The next serious test should not be another hand-designed edge case.

It should ask independent model implementations to classify the frozen regression set using only:

- canonical candidate text;
- case descriptions;
- no prior answer keys.

Measure:

- classification agreement;
- scope consistency;
- false-positive drift;
- false-negative drift;
- carrier stability;
- autonomy consistency.

A canon change should not depend on one model repeatedly interpreting its own prior language.

---

# 36. Real-world validation requirement

Before canon revision, use several cases not designed around the protocol.

Recommended domains:

- organisational decision process;
- technical control/allocation system;
- human decision process;
- biological selection/regulation process;
- scientific observational case.

The purpose is not breadth for its own sake.

The purpose is to detect hidden overfitting to the synthetic regression suite.

---

# 37. Backward-compatibility gate

For every proposed canonical clause, test:

> Does this invalidate previous v4.1 analyses that were correct under their evidence?

If yes:

classify the difference as:

- correction;
- intentional semantic change;
- incompatible revision.

No breaking change should be hidden inside “clarification.”

---

# 38. Publication-surface gate

Before publication, test whether the revised wording encourages unsupported claims such as:

- “algorithm has agency”;
- “deterministic selection proves autonomy”;
- “I-function means consciousness”;
- “selective architecture is a subject.”

If wording makes such misuse likely:

revise before canon freeze.

# 39. Canonical naming recommendation

Do **not** replace:

> I — Situated Selectivity

with:

- I-function;
- I-autonomy;
- I-position

as separate top-level operators.

The regression programme does not justify such fragmentation.

Instead preserve the canonical triad and use the experimental distinctions as internal analytical qualifiers.

This maintains conceptual continuity and reduces semantic surface.

# 44. Final comparison summary

The experimental programme does **not** overturn the core I·V·O architecture.

O remains Structured Possibility.

V remains Instantiated Relation.

I remains Situated Selectivity.

What changes is the internal logic of I.

The strongest supported correction is:

> **selection and autonomy should not be conflated.**

A selective function may be:

- fully mechanistically explained;
- deterministic;
- redundantly realised;
- partially causal;
- temporarily masked;

and still remain a selective function.

Autonomy is the separate question of whether structural openness remains after full O/V reconstruction.

Situatedness remains mandatory, but not as an independently established positional operator.

The regression programme therefore supports a targeted revision of the I-rule rather than a redesign of the entire Lens.
