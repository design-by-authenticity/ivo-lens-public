# T4 DECISION NOTE / v0.4 AMENDMENT PROPOSAL

## I·V·O Experimental Semantics

**Status:** EXPERIMENTAL / NON-CANONICAL  
**Applies to:** I-position / I-function / I-autonomy Protocol v0.4  
**Trigger:** T4 — I-position Boundary / Non-Redundant Situated Difference  
**Canonical baseline:** I·V·O Lens v4.1 remains frozen  
**Decision type:** experimental architecture amendment  
**Immediate canonical effect:** none

---

# 1. Purpose

This note records the architectural consequence of T4.

T4 was designed to test whether **I-position** can be maintained as an independent analytical dimension when O and V are given their strongest possible reconstruction, including:

- physical location;
- connectivity;
- asymmetric access;
- stored history;
- memory;
- adaptive baselines;
- internal state;
- relocation history;
- update rules;
- context-dependent registration.

The test did not establish a positive I-position that survived this full O/V exhaustion challenge.

The result therefore requires more than a local wording adjustment.

It requires reconsideration of whether **I-position should remain an independent experimental classification at all.**

---

# 2. Decision trigger

The decisive T4 result is:

> No tested case produced a regressively stable positive I-position once the full relevant O/V history, state, storage, coupling, and update structure was retained.

This result was strongest in Cases C and D.

Both cases initially appeared position-like because:

- identical present inputs could produce different registered relations;
- retained history mattered;
- relocation could preserve a history-conditioned difference even under equal current connectivity.

However, all such differences remained reconstructable through:

history  
+ stored state  
+ update rule  
+ present input  
+ current coupling

within O/V.

Removing the I-position label did not remove the relevant distinctions.

Therefore:

> the explanatory burden attributed to I-position was not shown to exceed the explanatory capacity of full O/V reconstruction.

---

# 3. Formal T4 decision

## Decision

**Independent positive I-position is NOT ESTABLISHED under the current experimental evidence.**

More specifically:

**I-position is downgraded from an active independent experimental classification to an unresolved research hypothesis.**

This means:

- I-position is no longer to be treated as a co-equal positive dimension alongside I-function and I-autonomy in the amended v0.4 architecture;
- no positive I-position status should be assigned unless a future case survives the full O/V exhaustion challenge;
- positional information remains analytically important, but is represented through ordinary analysis fields rather than a separate I-status.

---

# 4. What is not being concluded

This decision does **not** conclude that:

- position is meaningless;
- situatedness is irrelevant;
- access structure is irrelevant;
- history is irrelevant;
- observers never differ;
- perspective is unreal;
- all analytical viewpoints are interchangeable.

The conclusion is narrower:

> Current testing has not demonstrated that these differences require a separate positive I-position category once O/V is fully specified.

Situated differences may remain crucial to analysis while still being fully representable through:

- scope;
- carrier;
- access structure;
- history;
- current state;
- connectivity;
- outside-view constraints.

---

# 5. Revision of the experimental architecture

## Previous v0.4 architecture

v0.4 treated four dimensions as independently reportable:

1. I-position
2. I-function
3. Mechanistic implementation
4. I-autonomy

## Amended architecture

After T4, the preferred experimental architecture becomes:

1. **Situated analysis**
   - scope
   - carrier
   - access
   - history
   - outside view

2. **I-function**
   - positive selective function

3. **Mechanistic implementation**
   - how the function is realised

4. **I-autonomy**
   - residual structural openness after strongest adequate O/V reconstruction

The first category is analytical metadata / structure.

It is not, at present, an independent positive I-classification.

---

# 6. Status of I-position terminology

The term **I-position** may still be used in:

- historical regression records;
- discussion of the experimental hypothesis;
- cases explicitly designed to test whether independent positional status can be recovered.

However, in active v0.4 analyses after this amendment:

> `I-position` should not receive ESTABLISHED or SUPPORTED status as part of the main result schema.

Instead, record:

- carrier;
- access structure;
- history;
- scope;
- outside view;
- positional dependencies.

If a future case appears to require an independent I-position, classify it provisionally as:

> **POSITION HYPOTHESIS — CANDIDATE**

and subject it to the full O/V exhaustion challenge before any stronger status is allowed.

---

# 7. Full O/V exhaustion rule

The T4 result adds a mandatory rule.

Before any future independent position claim:

> Construct the strongest adequate O/V account including present and historical structure.

This must include where relevant:

- physical coupling;
- network topology;
- information access;
- memory;
- internal state;
- stored history;
- baselines;
- update rules;
- temporal dependencies;
- relocation trajectory;
- context-dependent mappings.

Then remove the position label.

Ask:

> Does any analytically relevant distinction disappear?

If no:

> no independent I-position is warranted.

If yes:

> specify exactly what is lost and why that loss cannot be represented within O/V.

Only then may a position hypothesis proceed.

---

# 8. New negative position rule

The strongest regressively supported position rule is now:

> **If full O/V reconstruction preserves all analytically relevant differences attributed to position, an independent I-position assignment is not warranted.**

This is stronger and more operational than:

> asymmetric access is insufficient.

It subsumes the earlier negative findings for:

- sensors;
- channels;
- network nodes;
- memory;
- local history;
- adaptive baselines;
- relocation persistence.

# 12. Consequence for I-function

This amendment does **not** weaken I-function.

In fact, T4 clarifies an asymmetry between function and position.

For I-function, regression testing has established that:

> complete mechanistic reconstruction does not revoke a positively evidenced selective function.

A selection can remain functionally real even when its implementation is fully carried by O/V.

For I-position, the equivalent claim has **not** been established.

So far:

> full O/V reconstruction appears capable of carrying all tested positional distinctions without requiring an additional positive I-position classification.

This asymmetry is accepted.

No symmetry between experimental subdimensions is required.

---

# 13. Consequence for I-autonomy

No change.

I-autonomy remains:

> residual structural openness after the strongest adequate O/V reconstruction.

T4 provides no evidence that history, memory, perspective, or positional difference creates autonomy.

Therefore:

- unknown ≠ autonomy;
- history ≠ autonomy;
- perspective ≠ autonomy;
- relocation ≠ autonomy.

# 14. Revised experimental decomposition

The amended experimental interpretation of I should now distinguish:

## A. Situated conditions of analysis

These specify:

- where the relevant distinction is instantiated;
- what the carrier can access;
- what history it carries;
- what remains outside view;
- at what scope the analysis is performed.

These conditions are mandatory but are not currently a separate I-status.

## B. I-function

A positively evidenced selective function that contributes to which continuation becomes realised within an explicitly declared analytical scope.

## C. I-autonomy

Residual structural openness after strongest adequate O/V reconstruction.

## D. Mechanistic implementation

The degree to which the relevant process is mechanistically reconstructed.

---

# 15. Revised role of “Situated” in Situated Selectivity

This amendment does not remove the word **Situated** from the canonical concept.

Canonical v4.1 remains untouched.

Experimentally, however, “situated” is provisionally treated as:

> a mandatory condition of how selective claims are scoped and carried

rather than:

> an independently scored operator subdimension.

Thus:

- selection is always instantiated somewhere;
- by some carrier;
- under some access/history constraints;
- within some scope.

But those conditions do not yet justify a separate positive `I-position` status.

# 23. Amendment decision

## Proposed decision

**ACCEPT AMENDMENT**

Rationale:

1. T4 failed to establish independent positive I-position.
2. Full O/V reconstruction preserved all tested position-like distinctions.
3. Retaining I-position as a mandatory positive dimension would risk reification.
4. Positional information remains available through scope, carrier, access, history, and outside-view analysis.
5. I-function and I-autonomy remain independently supported by regression.
6. Removing active I-position reduces publication surface and semantic complexity without losing tested explanatory content.

# 26. Future recovery condition for I-position

Independent I-position may be reconsidered only if a future case satisfies all of the following:

1. the case contains a stable position-like distinction;
2. current connectivity alone does not explain it;
3. history/state/memory do not exhaust it;
4. complete O/V reconstruction still loses a relevant analytical distinction when position is removed;
5. the lost distinction is operationally identifiable;
6. the result does not inflate sensors, channels, buffers, network nodes, or adaptive mappings into positions;
7. the finding replicates across at least two structurally different cases.

Until then:

> independent I-position remains an unresolved research hypothesis.

---

# 27. Final decision statement

T4 does not justify a positive independent I-position category.

The experimental architecture should therefore become more parsimonious.

The strongest supported structure is now:

> **Situated analysis provides scope, carrier, access, history, and outside-view constraints.**

> **I-function identifies positively evidenced selective contribution to a relevant continuation.**

> **Mechanistic implementation describes how that function is realised.**

> **I-autonomy tests whether structural openness remains after strongest adequate O/V reconstruction.**

The key negative rule is:

> **If full O/V reconstruction preserves every analytically relevant distinction attributed to position, independent I-position is not warranted.**

The key methodological consequence is:

> **Situatedness remains mandatory for analysis without being granted an independent operator status merely by description.**
