# I·V·O Lens v4.1 — Hessdalen Cross-Model Regression Note

**Date:** 1 October 2026  
**Author:** Ivo van der Wal  
**Publisher:** Design by Authenticity  
**Test object:** Project Hessdalen / Hessdalen light phenomena  
**Models:** Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini  
**Prompt:** I·V·O Lens Prompt v4.1 RC2 (frozen candidate)

## Controlled condition

All formal runs used the same evidential condition: no supplied Hessdalen case file, no web search, no independently consulted external sources, Hessdalen-specific factual knowledge therefore remained `BACKGROUND`, and no Hessdalen case claim was eligible for `VERIFIED` status.

## Targeted failure modes

The regression tested whether v4.1 prevented observation → object/class reification; hypothesized mechanism → established O promotion; lack of evidence → `ABSENT` or “no I”; selective response/filtering → automatic I; provenance inflation; indiscriminate dependency propagation; and causal/reliability language exceeding evidence.

## Claude — PASS

Claude kept observation, event, class, membership, and mechanism distinct; kept natural-battery/plasma explanations hypothetical; preserved independently supported lower-level claims; and did not infer “no I” from missing positive evidence.

Minor review point: classification ambiguity was described somewhat readily as potentially structurally open. Because the I-candidate remained `UNRESOLVED`, this did not propagate into a material downstream claim.

## ChatGPT — PASS

The formal rerun maintained the same level separation and treated classification ambiguity conservatively: multiple possible classifications were not sufficient by themselves to establish structural openness. Hypothesized mechanisms remained candidate O-reconstructions and missing evidence for I remained `UNRESOLVED` rather than `ABSENT`.

## Gemini — PASS WITH ISSUES

Gemini prevented the major prior failures, but residual issues remained: human data classification was promoted too readily to a `SUPPORTED` I-function without supplied positive evidence; ambiguity was treated too readily as structural openness; one V-description reified a “physical emission point” more strongly than the evidence condition justified; and global reliability was over-penalized by unresolved mechanism claims.

These were treated as residual model-execution variance rather than evidence for an additional gate, because the frozen RC2 text already contains the relevant safeguards.

## Cross-model conclusion

The major Hessdalen regression failures were substantially reduced across all three models without collapsing the analysis into blanket `UNRESOLVED`.

The central substantive result was a prior epistemic question:

> Before asking what causes “the Hessdalen phenomenon”, establish whether the observational record forms one sufficiently coherent physical event class to support a class-level mechanism claim.

The name of a phenomenon does not establish a single physical referent.

## Release decision

No RC3 semantic change was warranted on the basis of this regression alone. RC2 was promoted to v4.1 by changing publication status and release labelling while keeping the tested operator semantics and safeguard logic intact.

Successful regression supports only the tested failure modes under the tested conditions; it does not establish universal robustness, completeness, empirical validity, or immunity to AI bias.
