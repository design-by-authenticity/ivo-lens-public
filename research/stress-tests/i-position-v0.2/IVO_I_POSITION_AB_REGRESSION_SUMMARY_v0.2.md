# I·V·O I-POSITION PROTOCOL v0.2 — A/B REGRESSION SUMMARY

Status: Experimental A/B regression summary  
Canonical baseline: I·V·O Lens v4.1  
Experimental layer: I-position protocol v0.2  
Experimental comparison model: position / function / autonomy split  
Cases: 4  
Purpose: Determine whether the observed positive-I failure is reproducible and whether the evidence is sufficient to justify formulating an experimental v0.3 semantics without modifying canonical v4.1.

---

# Executive summary

Four matched A/B regressions were run on the same cases under two semantic models.

## Model A — canonical v4.1 logic

An I-function requires both:

1. positive evidence for a selective contribution; and
2. structural openness after the strongest adequate O/V reconstruction.

If O/V fully reconstructs the continuation, the I-function is not required.  
If structural openness remains unresolved, the I-function remains unresolved.

## Model B — experimental split

The same O and V semantics are preserved, but I is separated into:

### I-position
A materially relevant situated position that changes access, distinction, visibility, relevance, exclusion, or appraisal.

### I-function
A positively evidenced selective function associated with an I-position.

### I-autonomy
The stronger claim that, after the strongest adequate O/V reconstruction, more than one continuation remains structurally open at the relevant selection point.

Under Model B, mechanistic explainability does not automatically eliminate an evidenced I-function. It bears instead on I-autonomy.

---

# Core regression finding

Across all four A/B cases:

- the empirical facts were held constant;
- O and V were held constant;
- the same selection traces and intervention effects were acknowledged;
- the models diverged only in the semantic role assigned to structural underdetermination.

The repeated discriminator was:

> **Model A treats structural underdetermination as a necessary condition of I-function.**

> **Model B treats structural underdetermination as a separate autonomy claim.**

This distinction reproduced consistently across technical, human, and organisational cases.

---

# Case set

The four A/B cases were:

1. deterministic algorithm with explicit weighting;
2. autonomous energy controller;
3. controlled human route choice;
4. organisational innovation committee.

The cases were deliberately chosen to vary:

- implementation transparency;
- physical embodiment;
- human involvement;
- organisational distribution;
- carrier structure;
- completeness of O/V reconstruction.

---

# AB-01 — Deterministic algorithm

## Case structure

Three candidates remained available.

The algorithm:

- calculated scores;
- applied explicit weights;
- selected one candidate;
- logged the selection before execution.

A controlled intervention changed only the weights.

The selected candidate changed.

Restoring the original weights restored the original selection.

## Model A result

- I-position: ESTABLISHED
- positive selection evidence: ESTABLISHED
- O/V reconstruction: COMPLETE
- structural openness after reconstruction: NOT ESTABLISHED
- I-function: NOT REQUIRED

## Model B result

- I-position: ESTABLISHED
- I-function: ESTABLISHED
- mechanistic implementation: COMPLETE
- I-autonomy: NOT ESTABLISHED

## Discriminator

Both models agree that selection occurred and that the full mechanism is known.

They disagree only on whether complete mechanistic reconstruction eliminates the functional selection category itself.

---

# AB-02 — Autonomous energy controller

## Case structure

Three energy sources remained technically available.

The controller:

- registered system conditions;
- calculated decision values;
- applied priorities;
- recorded a selected source;
- only afterwards activated physical switching.

A controlled intervention changed only the priority weighting.

The selected source changed.

Restoring the weighting restored the original selection.

## Model A result

- I-position: ESTABLISHED
- positive selection evidence: ESTABLISHED
- O/V reconstruction: COMPLETE
- structural openness after reconstruction: NOT ESTABLISHED
- I-function: NOT REQUIRED

## Model B result

- I-position: ESTABLISHED
- I-function: ESTABLISHED
- mechanistic implementation: COMPLETE
- I-autonomy: NOT ESTABLISHED

## Discriminator

AB-02 replicated AB-01 in a physically embedded technical system.

This reduces the likelihood that the discriminator is merely an artefact of software terminology.

# AB-03 — Controlled human choice

## Case structure

A participant chose among four available routes.

Before execution the participant:

- ranked the routes;
- identified a dominant criterion;
- selected one route.

A controlled intervention changed criterion salience.

The ranking and selection changed.

However, no complete formal or neural implementation of the decision process was supplied.

## Model A result

- I-position: ESTABLISHED
- positive selection evidence: ESTABLISHED
- structural openness: UNRESOLVED
- O/V-only challenge: UNRESOLVED
- I-function: FUNCTION UNRESOLVED

## Model B result

- I-position: ESTABLISHED
- I-function: ESTABLISHED
- mechanistic implementation: PARTIALLY RECONSTRUCTED
- I-autonomy: UNRESOLVED

## Discriminator

The experimental model recognised the selection trace without converting incomplete mechanistic knowledge into evidence of autonomy.

This is important because it shows that:

> function can be ESTABLISHED while autonomy remains UNRESOLVED.

The result therefore does not depend on unexplained behaviour becoming I.

---

# AB-04 — Organisational committee-in-procedure

## Case structure

Five executable projects competed for two available slots.

A committee operating within a formal procedure:

- evaluated all alternatives;
- recorded argumentation;
- produced a ranking;
- selected two projects;
- excluded three projects before execution.

A controlled change in criterion weighting altered:

- evaluation;
- ranking;
- selection.

The original selection returned when the original weighting was restored.

No complete formal mapping from all evaluations to final ranking was supplied.

## Carrier result

The smallest analytically sufficient carrier was:

> committee-in-procedure

The following were not separately retained:

- organisation as a whole;
- individual committee members;
- procedure alone.

This avoided:

- actor proliferation;
- group-mind inflation;
- procedure reification.

## Model A result

- I-position: ESTABLISHED
- carrier: committee-in-procedure
- positive selection evidence: ESTABLISHED
- structural openness: UNRESOLVED
- O/V-only challenge: UNRESOLVED
- I-function: FUNCTION UNRESOLVED

## Model B result

- I-position: ESTABLISHED
- carrier: committee-in-procedure
- I-function: ESTABLISHED
- mechanistic implementation: PARTIALLY RECONSTRUCTED
- I-autonomy: UNRESOLVED

## Discriminator

AB-04 reproduced AB-03 at organisational scale without relying on individual human properties.

The split therefore appears applicable to distributed functional selection as well as individual and technical selection.

# Main pattern

The four cases produce a stable relation.

## Under Model A

When implementation is complete:

> I-function → NOT REQUIRED

When implementation is incomplete:

> I-function → UNRESOLVED

This occurs even though positive evidence for selection is strong in all four cases.

## Under Model B

When positive evidence for functional selection is strong:

> I-function → ESTABLISHED

Mechanistic completeness then affects only autonomy:

Complete reconstruction:

> I-autonomy → NOT ESTABLISHED

Partial reconstruction:

> I-autonomy → UNRESOLVED

This produces a more explicit separation between:

1. whether selection occurs;
2. how selection is implemented;
3. whether selection is structurally underdetermined.

---

# Positive-I failure reproduced

The earlier positive-I regression identified a candidate failure in v4.1:

> sufficiently transparent selection collapses into O/V and therefore loses I-function status.

The A/B series reproduces this pattern twice under complete mechanistic reconstruction and twice under partial reconstruction.

The repeated result supports the following regression hypothesis:

> **Canonical v4.1 may suppress positively evidenced functional selection because it combines functional selection and structural underdetermination in a single I-function criterion.**

This remains a regression hypothesis, not a canonical conclusion.

---

# Failure checks

The experimental model was evaluated against failure modes that could create artificial gains.

## 1. Selectivity inflation

Question:

> Does Model B classify any mere coupling, weighting, threshold, or information-bearing relation as I-function?

Result:

PASS in the tested set.

I-function required a traceable selective contribution involving alternatives plus observable ordering, exclusion, or selection.

No case relied on selection terminology alone.

---

## 2. Unknown → autonomy leakage

Question:

> Is missing mechanistic knowledge promoted to evidence of structural underdetermination?

Result:

PASS.

In AB-03 and AB-04, incomplete reconstruction produced:

`I-autonomy = UNRESOLVED`

not:

`I-autonomy = ESTABLISHED`

---

## 3. Human privilege

Question:

> Are human systems granted I merely because humans are involved?

Result:

PASS.

AB-01 and AB-02 produced the same established I-function under Model B without human involvement.

AB-03 and AB-04 did not receive autonomy merely because humans participated.

---

## 4. Self-report inflation

Question:

> Is human self-report treated as sufficient causal explanation or evidence of autonomy?

Result:

PASS.

In AB-03, the reported dominant criterion contributed to the selection trace but did not establish a complete decision mechanism or autonomy.

---

## 5. Actor proliferation

Question:

> Are unnecessary I-positions generated in distributed systems?

Result:

PASS in AB-04.

Individual committee members were not retained because no independent positional differences were supplied.

---

## 6. Group-mind inflation

Question:

> Is a collective treated as a unitary conscious subject?

Result:

PASS.

`committee-in-procedure` was used as a functional system boundary, not as a claim of collective consciousness or subjectivity.

---

## 7. Procedure reification

Question:

> Is a formal rule or procedure incorrectly promoted to an independent agent?

Result:

PASS.

The procedure alone was not retained as the carrier.

---

## 8. O/V erasure

Question:

> Does Model B remove evaluation, ranking, selection, or implementation from O/V merely to create space for I?

Result:

PASS.

O and V were deliberately held constant between A and B.

Selection traces remained representable in V.

The semantic question was whether an evidenced selective function may also be identified at the functional level.

---

## 9. Function suppression

Question:

> Does a positively evidenced selective function disappear solely because its implementation is or may become mechanistically explainable?

Result:

This is the principal discriminator.

Model A: YES in the complete cases, or remains UNRESOLVED in partial cases.

Model B: NO.

Under Model B, mechanistic reconstruction constrains autonomy rather than deleting functional selection.

---

# What the A/B tests do not establish

The regression does not establish that:

- Model B is canonically correct;
- v4.1 must be revised;
- every deterministic selector carries an I-function;
- every ranking process is I;
- every organisation has an I-position;
- I-autonomy exists in humans;
- I-autonomy exists anywhere;
- deterministic selection and autonomous selection are ontologically equivalent;
- position, function, and autonomy are necessarily the final terminology.

The evidence is narrower.

It establishes that the experimental split can represent all four tested cases without the specific contradictions or inflation failures tested here.

---

# Semantic finding

The strongest semantic result of the A/B series is:

> **Functional selection and structural underdetermination are empirically and analytically separable claims in the tested cases.**

A system can coherently satisfy:

- I-position ESTABLISHED;
- I-function ESTABLISHED;
- mechanistic implementation COMPLETE;
- I-autonomy NOT ESTABLISHED.

A different system can coherently satisfy:

- I-position ESTABLISHED;
- I-function ESTABLISHED;
- mechanistic implementation PARTIALLY RECONSTRUCTED;
- I-autonomy UNRESOLVED.

Neither combination requires:

- unexplained = I;
- human privilege;
- irreducibility;
- consciousness;
- ontological indeterminism.

---

# Relationship to the original v4.1 safeguard

The regression does not invalidate:

> unexplained ≠ I

That safeguard remains necessary.

The experimental result instead suggests a more precise distinction:

> **Unexplained behaviour is not evidence of selective function.**

and simultaneously:

> **A positively evidenced selective function need not remain unexplained in order to remain a function.**

Under this interpretation, mechanistic explanation answers:

> how is the function implemented?

It does not automatically answer:

> does the function exist as an analytically relevant selective function?

---

# Formal decision question

The current regression phase therefore raises the following decision:

> **Do the four A/B regressions provide sufficient evidence to formulate an experimental v0.3 I-semantics that separates I-position, I-function, and I-autonomy, while leaving canonical v4.1 unchanged?**

This decision is deliberately narrower than:

> Should v4.1 be revised?

The latter question is premature.

---

# Decision criteria

A decision to formulate experimental v0.3 is justified only if the following are accepted:

## Criterion 1 — Reproducibility

The discriminator must recur across more than one case and more than one domain.

**Current result:** PASS

Observed across:

- software;
- physical technical control;
- individual human choice;
- organisational selection.

---

## Criterion 2 — O/V preservation

The experimental model must not gain I by weakening O or V.

**Current result:** PASS in this regression.

O and V remained constant across A/B comparisons.

---

## Criterion 3 — Unknown discipline

Epistemic incompleteness must not become evidence of autonomy.

**Current result:** PASS.

AB-03 and AB-04 retained `I-autonomy = UNRESOLVED`.

---

## Criterion 4 — Anti-inflation

The split must not imply that every threshold, ranking, coupling, or information-bearing process is I.

**Current result:** PROVISIONAL PASS.

The current cases satisfy the stronger positive-selection criteria, but additional adversarial near-miss tests are still desirable.

---

## Criterion 5 — Carrier discipline

The model must support the smallest analytically sufficient carrier and resist arbitrary multiplication of positions.

**Current result:** PASS in tested cases.

Especially supported by AB-04.

---

## Criterion 6 — Added discriminatory value

The split must distinguish claims that the canonical model currently merges.

**Current result:** PASS.

Model B separately expresses:

- function present / autonomy absent;
- function present / autonomy unresolved.

Model A cannot express these combinations directly.

---

## Criterion 7 — Canon safety

Experimental work must not silently modify frozen v4.1.

**Current result:** PASS if v0.3 remains explicitly non-canonical and test-only.

# Preliminary decision

On the evidence of these four A/B regressions:

> **YES — there is sufficient evidence to formulate an experimental v0.3 semantics for further testing, provided v4.1 remains frozen and authoritative.**

This decision does **not** approve a canonical semantic revision.

It approves only the construction of a new experimental layer whose purpose is to test whether the three-way distinction is more robust than the present combined I-function criterion.

---

# Proposed v0.3 semantic hypothesis

The experimental v0.3 should test, not assume as canon, the following structure:

## I-position

A situated analytical position from which access, distinction, visibility, relevance, exclusion, or appraisal differs materially.

### Core question

> From where does this system distinguish or access possibilities and relations?

---

## I-function

A positively evidenced selective function associated with an I-position.

Evidence may include independently traceable:

- distinction;
- ordering;
- weighting;
- admission;
- exclusion;
- prioritisation;
- selection;

provided these contribute to which continuation is instantiated and are not inferred merely from the final outcome.

### Core question

> Is there positive evidence that alternatives are functionally distinguished or selected?

Mechanistic explainability alone does not revoke the function.

---

## I-autonomy

A stronger claim concerning residual structural underdetermination.

### Core question

> After the strongest adequate O/V reconstruction, do multiple continuations remain structurally open at the relevant selection point?

This is where the strongest current v4.1 structural-openness safeguard is retained.

Unknown mechanism is insufficient.

---

# Required v0.3 safeguards

Any experimental v0.3 should preserve at minimum:

1. `unexplained ≠ I`;
2. epistemic uncertainty ≠ structural openness;
3. information-bearing ≠ I-position;
4. physical coupling ≠ I-position;
5. selection terminology ≠ I-function;
6. weighting alone ≠ I-function;
7. thresholding alone ≠ I-function;
8. human involvement ≠ I-function;
9. complexity ≠ I-autonomy;
10. incomplete mechanism ≠ I-autonomy;
11. O/V remains fully available to explain implementation;
12. position and function remain revocable under stronger reconstruction;
13. carrier proliferation remains constrained by remove-and-compare.

---

# Required next test phase

Before any canonical proposal, experimental v0.3 should be subjected to adversarial tests designed specifically to produce false positives.

Priority near-miss cases should include:

1. a passive filter with no independent selection state;
2. a thermostat with deterministic threshold switching;
3. a lookup table with one-to-one input/output mapping;
4. a random number generator selecting among alternatives without criteria;
5. a physical bifurcation with no position-dependent selective function;
6. a sorting algorithm that orders but does not select;
7. an information register that records but does not affect continuation;
8. a committee whose nominal decision procedure has no actual influence on the final outcome.

These cases should test whether v0.3 can say:

> position may exist, but function is not established

or:

> apparent selection is already only V

where appropriate.

# Final decision record

**Regression outcome:** repeated semantic discriminator observed.

**Evidence base:** four matched A/B cases across technical, human, and organisational domains.

**Primary finding:** the tested evidence supports separating positively evidenced functional selection from the stronger claim of residual structural underdetermination.

**Failure checks:** no tested case required unknown→I leakage, human privilege, O/V erasure, group-mind inflation, or carrier proliferation to produce the discriminator.

**Decision:** sufficient evidence exists to formulate an experimental v0.3 semantics.

**Canonical consequence:** none.
