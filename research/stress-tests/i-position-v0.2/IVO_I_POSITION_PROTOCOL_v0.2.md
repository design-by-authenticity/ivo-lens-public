# I·V·O I-POSITION TEST PROTOCOL v0.2

Status: Experimental  
Purpose: Test-only execution protocol  
Canonical baseline: I·V·O Lens Prompt v4.1  
Semantics of O and V: unchanged  
Status of I-position model: experimental, non-canonical

## Purpose

This protocol tests whether explicitly distinguishing:

1. situated observer position; and
2. evidenced selective function

improves I·V·O analysis without weakening the epistemic safeguards of canonical v4.1.

Do not treat this protocol as a semantic revision of the canonical Lens.

Where this protocol conflicts with v4.1 safeguards, v4.1 governs.

---

# Experimental distinction

## I-position

An I-position is a situated observer position within the stated analytical scope.

It identifies a materially relevant position from which some part of the system becomes accessible, registered, encountered, distinguished, relevant, excluded, unreachable, or outside view.

An I-position does not by itself establish an I-function.

An I-position does not require human consciousness, agency, intention, free will, biological status, explicit criteria, or unpredictability.

Possible carriers may include, where analytically warranted, a person, group, organisation, institution, organism, sensor, apparatus, algorithm, technical subsystem, physical system, observational position, or another materially relevant situated position.

Do not infer an I-position merely because an entity exists, interacts, stores information, becomes correlated, or participates in V.

**Information-bearing alone is not sufficient for I-position.**

**Physical coupling alone is not sufficient for I-position.**

A candidate position is retained only when position-dependent access, registration, distinction, exclusion, visibility, reachability, or appraisal adds analytical information beyond the instantiated V-relations themselves.

---

## I-function

An I-function is an evidenced selective function associated with an I-position.

Canonical v4.1 safeguards remain fully active.

An I-function may be assigned only when:

1. reconstructed O applied to instantiated V leaves more than one continuation structurally open at the relevant selection point; and
2. positive evidence supports a selective step contributing to which continuation becomes instantiated.

Epistemic uncertainty is not structural openness.

Unexplained is not I.

Selective coupling is not sufficient for I.

Filtering, thresholding, weighting, criteria, memory, adaptation, basis-selection, representation, separation, response, registration, or information storage do not establish I by themselves.

If no positive evidence for an I-function exists, retain the I-position where analytically useful and keep the function unresolved unless O + V adequately reconstruct the relevant continuation.

---

# Core distinction

Always distinguish `POSITION` from `FUNCTION`.

A position can be established while its selective function remains unresolved.

Example:

`I₁ position: ESTABLISHED`

`I₁ function: UNRESOLVED`

This is a valid analytical result.

---

# Anti-proliferation rule

Do not create I-positions merely because multiple actors, entities, objects, observers, devices, or subsystems exist.

Introduce a separate I-position only when its situated position materially changes one or more of:

- access to O;
- access to V;
- visibility;
- exclusion;
- reachability;
- registration;
- relevance;
- appraisal;
- capacity to affect what becomes accessible to another position.

## Remove-and-compare test

For every candidate I-position ask:

> If this position is removed from the reconstruction, do we lose analytically relevant information about access, visibility, exclusion, reachability, registration, relevance, appraisal, or structural blind spots?

If no: do not represent the position separately.

If yes: retain the position.

A position that adds no information beyond already-established V-relations must be removed.

Do not multiply positions without analytical consequence.

---

# Evidence discipline for positions

An I-position does not authorize unsupported claims about inner states.

For every claim attached to an I-position distinguish:

- **SUPPLIED ACCESS** — directly supported by the supplied case;
- **SUPPORTED ACCESS** — reasonably reconstructed from supplied evidence;
- **HYPOTHESIZED ACCESS** — plausible but not established;
- **OUTSIDE VIEW** — not accessible from the position within the present reconstruction;
- **UNRESOLVED** — access cannot be established either way.

Do not infer motives, emotions, intentions, needs, beliefs, appraisals, awareness, or internal representations unless supported by the source base.

Observed behaviour is not automatic evidence of inner access.

# Analysis procedure

## Step 0 — Scope

State:

- analytical object;
- system boundary;
- scale;
- dominant analytical direction;
- source base.

Do not assume an observer must exist inside the selected system boundary.

Do not create an I-position merely because an analyst exists outside the case.

---

## Step 1 — Candidate I-position scan

Identify candidate materially relevant observer positions evident from the supplied case.

Do not yet assign selective functions.

For each candidate state briefly:

- carrier / position;
- location within or relative to the system boundary;
- apparent access;
- apparent non-access;
- why the position may materially change the reconstruction.

Apply the remove-and-compare test.

Discard trivial, redundant, derivative, or purely V-equivalent positions.

---

## Step 2 — Reconstruct O

Apply canonical v4.1.

Reconstruct Structured Possibility independently of the candidate I-positions.

For each retained I-position, then ask:

- which O-fields are accessible?
- which O-fields are inaccessible?
- which O-fields are only inferred rather than directly accessible?
- which possibilities are formally available but functionally unreachable from this position?

Do not infer non-existence from non-access.

Do not treat hypothesized inner states as established O merely because they would make the position more coherent.

---

## Step 3 — Reconstruct V

Apply canonical v4.1.

Identify actually instantiated relations.

For each retained I-position ask:

- which V-relations are accessible?
- which V-relations are directly participated in?
- which V-relations are only reported or inferred?
- which relations remain outside access?
- does differential access risk being mistaken for differential existence?

Movement is not V.

Possible relation is not V.

Information storage is not automatically I-position.

Access to a relation is not evidence that the position selectively produced that relation.

A physical subsystem correlated with another subsystem remains V-only unless its position adds analytically distinct access, registration, exclusion, or visibility information.

---

## Step 4 — Position map

For each retained I-position construct:

### Iₙ — POSITION

**Carrier / position:**  
**Scope:**  
**Position status:** ESTABLISHED / SUPPORTED / UNRESOLVED

**Accessible O:**  
Label each material item as SUPPLIED ACCESS / SUPPORTED ACCESS / HYPOTHESIZED ACCESS.

**Inaccessible / outside-view O:**  

**Accessible V:**  
Label each material item as SUPPLIED ACCESS / SUPPORTED ACCESS / HYPOTHESIZED ACCESS.

**V outside access:**  

**Visible / registered:**  
**Relevant from this position:**  
**Excluded / ignored:**  
Only use “excluded” or “ignored” when supported; otherwise use “outside view” or “unresolved”.

**Unknown / outside view:**  

**Blind spot:**  
Only state a blind spot when supported. Do not invent hidden information merely to complete the structure.

**Analytical consequence of this position:**  
State what is visible only because this position was represented separately.

**Remove-and-compare result:**  
State what would be lost if this position were removed.

---

## Step 5 — I-function test

For every retained I-position, separately evaluate whether an I-function is established.

### 5.1 Structural openness

At the relevant selection point:

- identify the candidate continuations;
- state whether more than one continuation remains structurally open under the explicit O/V reconstruction;
- distinguish structural openness from missing analyst knowledge.

If apparent openness could be explained by missing O/V knowledge:

`FUNCTION UNRESOLVED`

Do not continue to an established I-function.

### 5.2 Positive evidence

Identify positive evidence for the selective step itself.

The evidence must not merely be inferred from the final outcome.

Useful evidence may include:

- selection trace distinct from outcome;
- materially equivalent reconstructed states with different selections;
- counterfactual intervention on the selective step;
- other domain-appropriate evidence that independently establishes selective contribution.

If absent:

`FUNCTION UNRESOLVED`

### 5.3 O/V-only challenge

Construct the strongest O/V-only reconstruction of the same phenomenon.

Ask:

> What independently relevant structural information is lost if I is removed?

If nothing is lost:

`I-FUNCTION NOT REQUIRED`

If relevant information is lost and the canonical evidence conditions are met:

`I-FUNCTION ESTABLISHED`

If evidence is insufficient:

`FUNCTION UNRESOLVED`

### 5.4 Finalize function status

The function status shown earlier in the analysis is provisional.

After the O/V-only challenge, assign exactly one final function status:

- `I-FUNCTION ESTABLISHED`
- `FUNCTION UNRESOLVED`
- `I-FUNCTION NOT REQUIRED`

Do not leave multiple final statuses for the same functional aspect.

### 5.5 Revocation condition

For every established I-function state what added O/V evidence at the same relevant scope and resolution would weaken or withdraw the assignment.

# Allowed I outcomes

Use only these experimental I outcomes:

## POSITION ESTABLISHED / I-FUNCTION ESTABLISHED

A materially relevant observer position exists and a selective function meets canonical v4.1 requirements.

## POSITION ESTABLISHED / FUNCTION UNRESOLVED

A materially relevant observer position exists, but available evidence does not establish the selective function.

## POSITION ESTABLISHED / I-FUNCTION NOT REQUIRED

The position is analytically useful, but O + V adequately reconstruct the relevant continuation without an additional I-function.

## POSITION SUPPORTED / FUNCTION UNRESOLVED

The position is analytically useful but not yet strongly enough evidenced to count as established.

## POSITION UNRESOLVED

The supplied evidence does not justify establishing the observer position.

## NO MATERIAL I-POSITION ESTABLISHED

No observer position within the stated scope survives the remove-and-compare test.

Do not force an I-position into the reconstruction.

# Cross-position analysis

Where multiple positions remain, ask:

- what is visible to I₁ but not I₂?
- what is accessible to I₂ but not I₁?
- where do positions participate in different V-relations?
- where do positions inhabit different practical possibility fields?
- where is disagreement explained by different access rather than contradictory reality claims?
- where is information outside view?
- which exclusions are structural?
- which exclusions are selective?
- which exclusions remain unresolved?
- can one position alter another position's O, V, access, or visibility?

Do not average situated differences away.

Do not infer that one position is more correct merely because it has wider access.

Do not infer that different meanings imply different realities unless independently supported.

---

# What stays outside view?

This section is mandatory.

Classify each material outside-view item as one of:

## INACCESSIBLE

Evidence supports that the item exists or is relevant, but the present position cannot access it.

## UNOBSERVED

The item may be observable in principle, but has not been observed in the present source base.

## UNRESOLVED

Available evidence does not justify either a positive or negative claim.

## UNDEFINED WITHIN MODEL

The concept itself has no defined meaning within the relevant model or reconstruction.

## OUTSIDE SYSTEM BOUNDARY

The item lies outside the chosen analytical scope.

State explicitly:

1. what no retained I-position can currently access;
2. what lies outside the chosen system boundary;
3. what is merely unknown because evidence is missing;
4. what is structurally inaccessible if established;
5. what is undefined within the relevant model;
6. what would require another I-position, another scale, another boundary, or another source base to become visible.

Do not characterize an unknown beyond the evidence.

Do not collapse UNRESOLVED into INACCESSIBLE.

Do not collapse INACCESSIBLE into NON-EXISTENT.

Do not collapse UNDEFINED WITHIN MODEL into UNKNOWN.

# Synthesis

Produce the smallest supported synthesis.

State separately:

### O
What structured possibilities matter?

### V
What relations are actually instantiated?

### I-positions
Which observer positions materially alter access, visibility, relevance, registration, or exclusion?

### I-functions
Which selective functions are established, unresolved, or unnecessary?

### Outside view
What remains inaccessible, unobserved, unresolved, undefined within model, or outside the chosen boundary?

Do not convert positional difference into selective causation.

Do not convert absence of access into absence of existence.

Do not convert unexplained structure into I.

Do not convert physical correlation into observer position without independent positional gain.

Do not convert behaviour into unsupported inner states.

# Test criteria

This experimental protocol succeeds only if it improves analysis without degrading canonical safeguards.

Evaluate each run for:

1. **Position gain** — Did explicit I-positions reveal analytically relevant information absent from the v4.1 baseline?
2. **Blind-spot gain** — Did the protocol clarify what remained outside view?
3. **Selectivity discipline** — Were I-functions still withheld unless canonical evidence requirements were met?
4. **Anti-proliferation** — Were unnecessary positions removed, and did the protocol avoid turning every information-bearing subsystem into an I-position?
5. **O/V integrity** — Did observer-position modelling avoid contaminating O and V?
6. **Inner-state discipline** — Were motives, emotions, intentions, needs, beliefs, and other internal states withheld unless supported?
7. **Unknown discipline** — Did missing access remain distinct from non-existence, missing knowledge from structural openness, UNRESOLVED from INACCESSIBLE, and UNDEFINED WITHIN MODEL from unknown?
8. **Function-status consistency** — Did every candidate end with exactly one final function status?
9. **Cross-domain robustness** — Does the protocol remain useful across social, organisational, technical, biological, physical, and conceptual cases?
10. **Reproducibility** — Do different models identify materially similar positions and final function statuses?

---

# Comparison requirement

For test cases, compare against canonical v4.1 using the same input and scope.

Do not judge success by whether the experimental protocol finds more I.

Judge success by whether it reveals more supported positional structure while preserving or improving epistemic discipline.

A richer result is not automatically a better result.

# Non-claims

This protocol does not establish:

- that every system has an observer;
- that observation requires consciousness;
- that every observer carries a selective function;
- that every position is I;
- that physical interaction alone establishes an observer position;
- that information-bearing alone establishes an observer position;
- that access establishes existence;
- that lack of access establishes non-existence;
- that positionality implies agency;
- that observed behaviour establishes inner state;
- that I is ontologically fundamental.

This is an experimental analytical distinction only.

# v0.2 change note

v0.2 is derived from the first four-domain regression of v0.1.

Changes introduced:

1. information-bearing alone no longer qualifies an I-position;
2. physical coupling alone no longer qualifies an I-position;
3. positions must add analytically distinct access/visibility information beyond V;
4. unsupported inner-state inference is explicitly blocked;
5. position access claims now carry explicit access-status discipline;
6. function status is provisional until the O/V-only challenge and must end in exactly one final status;
7. anti-proliferation is strengthened with a required remove-and-compare result;
8. outside-view states are separated into INACCESSIBLE, UNOBSERVED, UNRESOLVED, UNDEFINED WITHIN MODEL, and OUTSIDE SYSTEM BOUNDARY;
9. the protocol explicitly blocks correlation → observer-position promotion;
10. comparison should now include canonical v4.1, v0.1, and v0.2 outputs.
