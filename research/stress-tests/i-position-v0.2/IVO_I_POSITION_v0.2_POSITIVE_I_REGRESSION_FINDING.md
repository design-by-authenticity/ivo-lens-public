# I·V·O I-POSITION PROTOCOL v0.2 — POSITIVE-I REGRESSION FINDING

Status: Experimental regression finding  
Protocol under test: I·V·O I-Position Test Protocol v0.2  
Canonical baseline: I·V·O Lens Prompt v4.1  
Test phase: Positive-I stress tests  
Cases: 4  
Purpose: Determine whether v4.1 + v0.2 can recognise an I-function when positive evidence for an explicit selective step is deliberately present.

---

# Executive finding

The positive-I regression produced a consistent and potentially significant failure pattern:

> **No tested case produced `I-FUNCTION ESTABLISHED`, including cases deliberately constructed with explicit alternatives, a traceable selection step, pre-outcome selection records, and controlled intervention on the selection process.**

The four positive-I cases produced:

- deterministic algorithm: `I-FUNCTION NOT REQUIRED`
- controlled human choice: `FUNCTION UNRESOLVED`
- organisational committee: `FUNCTION UNRESOLVED`
- autonomous energy controller: `I-FUNCTION NOT REQUIRED`

This suggests a possible false-negative failure mode in the current v4.1 definition of I.

The issue is not that v4.1 fails to detect evidence of selection.

The issue is that sufficiently well-reconstructed selection tends to collapse back into O and V, causing the I-function to disappear precisely when the selective process becomes most transparent.

---

# Background

The first v0.2 regression set focused mainly on restraint.

Those cases tested whether the protocol could:

- represent I-positions without inventing I-functions;
- prevent unexplained phenomena from becoming I;
- avoid position proliferation;
- preserve O/V integrity;
- maintain uncertainty where evidence was insufficient.

That regression succeeded provisionally.

The principal finding was:

> **position gain without selectivity inflation**

However, all four initial regression cases lacked a clearly established positive I-function.

The next regression phase was therefore designed to test the opposite failure mode:

> **Can the protocol recognise a genuine selective function when strong positive evidence for selection is deliberately supplied?**

---

# Positive-I test design

Four cases were constructed across different domains.

Each case deliberately increased the evidential strength for selection.

The test set included combinations of:

- multiple available alternatives;
- explicit accessibility of alternatives;
- a selection step distinct from final execution;
- pre-outcome selection traces;
- explicit criteria or weighting;
- controlled intervention on the selective process;
- changed continuation after intervention;
- restoration of the original continuation when the original selection condition was restored.

The aim was not to assume that these conditions automatically establish I.

The aim was to test whether any realistic evidence pattern could satisfy the current v4.1 criteria.

---

# Case 1 — Deterministic algorithm with explicit weighting

## Structure

Three candidates were available:

- A
- B
- C

All three satisfied entry conditions and remained executable.

The algorithm:

1. calculated scores;
2. applied explicit criterion weights;
3. recorded the selected candidate;
4. executed the selected candidate.

The selection trace existed independently of final execution.

A controlled intervention altered only the criterion weights.

The selected candidate changed accordingly.

When the original weights were restored, the original selection returned.

---

## Result

### I-position

`POSITION ESTABLISHED`

The algorithmic selection module added analytically relevant information about:

- available candidates;
- scoring;
- weighting;
- ranking;
- selection before execution.

### I-function

`I-FUNCTION NOT REQUIRED`

---

## Reason

Once the full scoring function, weights, criteria, and decision rule were included in O and V, the outcome became fully determined.

The strongest O/V-only reconstruction preserved all observed information.

Therefore:

- multiple alternatives existed in O;
- a selection trace was directly observed;
- intervention on the selection rule changed the outcome;
- but no structural underdetermination remained after full reconstruction.

The candidate I-function was therefore rejected.

---

# Case 2 — Controlled human choice

## Structure

A participant received four available routes.

All four were allowed and reachable.

Before execution the participant:

1. ranked all routes;
2. identified a dominant criterion;
3. selected one route.

A controlled intervention altered criterion salience.

The ranking changed.

The selected route changed.

The ranking existed as a trace before execution.

---

## Result

### I-position

`POSITION ESTABLISHED`

### I-function

`FUNCTION UNRESOLVED`

---

## Reason

The case contained stronger positive evidence for a selective step than earlier regressions:

- alternatives were explicit;
- ranking preceded execution;
- criterion weighting was recorded;
- intervention changed the ranking and choice.

However, unlike the deterministic algorithm, the full decision mechanism was not known.

Because a richer O/V reconstruction might eventually explain the choice completely, the I-function could not be established.

The function remained unresolved.

---

# Case 3 — Organisational innovation committee

## Structure

Five executable projects competed for funding for two available slots.

The organisation used:

- formal criteria;
- explicit evaluation;
- recorded argumentation;
- ranking;
- selection;
- exclusion before execution.

A controlled change in criterion weighting altered the ranking and selected projects.

---

## Result

### I-position

`POSITION ESTABLISHED`

for the smallest analytically useful carrier:

> committee-in-procedure

The organisation as a whole and individual committee members were not retained separately because they added no independent positional gain.

### I-function

`FUNCTION UNRESOLVED`

---

## Reason

The committee case contained:

- multiple open alternatives;
- explicit selection traces;
- explicit exclusions;
- criterion-sensitive ranking;
- intervention on the decision structure;
- changed continuation after intervention.

However, the procedure was not formally specified enough to exclude the possibility that a richer O/V reconstruction would fully determine the result.

Therefore the I-function remained unresolved.

---

# Case 4 — Autonomous energy controller

## Structure

An autonomous system selected one of three available energy sources:

- solar;
- battery;
- grid.

The controller recorded:

- system conditions;
- decision values;
- priority structure;
- selected source;
- command register;
- later physical switching.

The selection event was recorded before execution.

A controlled intervention changed only the priority weighting.

The selected source changed while the physical system state remained constant.

---

## Result

### I-position

`POSITION ESTABLISHED`

for the controller + selection register.

### I-function

`I-FUNCTION NOT REQUIRED`

---

## Reason

The case contained strong positive evidence of a distinct selection stage.

However, once the full controller logic, priorities, decision values, and state were reconstructed, exactly one outcome followed.

The O/V-only reconstruction retained the complete causal and functional chain:

`available sources → priority structure → decision values → selected source → execution`

No independent structural information was lost by removing I.

Therefore the I-function was rejected.

# Core failure pattern

The regression reveals a possible structural problem in the present v4.1 criteria.

The logic currently behaves approximately as follows:

1. I requires multiple structurally open continuations.
2. I also requires positive evidence for the selective step.
3. Positive evidence improves reconstruction of the selective mechanism.
4. A sufficiently complete reconstruction moves the selective mechanism into O and V.
5. Once O + V fully determine the continuation, structural underdetermination disappears.
6. Without structural underdetermination, I cannot be established.

This creates a possible evidential inversion:

> **The better the selection mechanism is understood, the less likely it becomes to qualify as I.**

---

# Transparency paradox

The strongest positive-I cases produced the weakest I status.

The two most transparent cases were:

- deterministic algorithm;
- autonomous energy controller.

Both had:

- explicit alternatives;
- explicit selection traces;
- criterion-sensitive behaviour;
- controlled interventions;
- reversible outcome changes.

Yet both produced:

`I-FUNCTION NOT REQUIRED`

because the mechanism was sufficiently transparent to reconstruct under O/V.

The less transparent cases were:

- human participant;
- organisational committee.

These produced:

`FUNCTION UNRESOLVED`

precisely because some of the internal selection process remained unknown.

This produces the following pattern:

| Reconstruction completeness | I result |
|---|---|
| Highly complete | NOT REQUIRED |
| Partially complete | UNRESOLVED |
| Poorly complete | UNRESOLVED |

The present regression found no clear route to:

`I-FUNCTION ESTABLISHED`

---

# Why this matters

This result may indicate that two analytically different questions are currently coupled too tightly in v4.1.

## Question A

Is there an evidenced selective function?

## Question B

Is that selective function structurally underdetermined or irreducible relative to O/V?

These are not necessarily the same question.

A system may demonstrably:

- compare alternatives;
- rank them;
- exclude some;
- retain others;
- select a continuation;
- produce a selection trace distinct from execution;
- change its selection under intervention;

while still being mechanistically explainable.

Mechanistic explainability does not necessarily erase the functional fact of selection.

---

# Candidate conceptual distinction

The regression suggests that future work may need to distinguish three layers:

## 1. I-position

A materially relevant situated observer position.

Question:

> From where does access, distinction, visibility, relevance, or exclusion differ?

---

## 2. I-function

An evidenced selective function associated with an I-position.

Question:

> Is there positive evidence that alternatives are actually distinguished, prioritised, admitted, excluded, or selected?

This level need not imply irreducibility.

---

## 3. I-autonomy / irreducibility

A stronger claim.

Question:

> Does the selective function remain structurally underdetermined after the strongest adequate O/V reconstruction?

This is where the current v4.1 structural-underdetermination requirement may belong more naturally.

This third layer may remain:

- ESTABLISHED;
- SUPPORTED;
- UNRESOLVED;
- NOT ESTABLISHED.

No such change is adopted by this finding.

It is recorded only as a candidate reconstruction motivated by the observed regression pattern.

---

# Relationship to “unexplained ≠ I”

This regression does **not** justify weakening the canonical safeguard:

> unexplained ≠ I

The opposite lesson may be more appropriate.

An I-function should require **positive evidence of selection**.

However, positive evidence of selection should not necessarily require the selection to remain unexplained after reconstruction.

The distinction may therefore be:

> unexplained behaviour is not evidence of I

while simultaneously:

> explained selective behaviour may still be a genuine selective function

These claims are compatible.

---

# Relationship to O/V integrity

This finding does not imply that O and V should stop explaining implementation.

A selective function may still be implemented entirely through O/V structure.

The open conceptual question is whether:

> implementation by O/V

should imply:

> elimination of the I-function

or merely:

> explanation of how the I-function is implemented.

The positive-I regression suggests that automatic elimination may be too strong.

---

# False-negative hypothesis

The regression therefore supports the following hypothesis:

> **v4.1 may contain a false-negative bias for I-functions.**

More specifically:

> When a selective process is sufficiently transparent to be reconstructed mechanistically, the current structural-underdetermination requirement can force the process back into O/V and prevent `I-FUNCTION ESTABLISHED`, even when selection itself is positively evidenced.

This remains a regression finding, not yet a canonical conclusion.

---

# Scope of the finding

The result is based on four deliberately constructed positive-I cases.

It does not establish:

- that v4.1 is generally invalid;
- that structural underdetermination is irrelevant;
- that all selection should count as I;
- that deterministic algorithms automatically carry I;
- that human choice implies irreducibility;
- that institutional decision-making is ontologically distinct from O/V;
- that the proposed position/function/autonomy split is already correct.

It establishes only that:

> under the tested conditions, no route to `I-FUNCTION ESTABLISHED` was found, including under unusually strong positive evidence for a distinct selection step.

---

# Regression criterion triggered

The canonical v4.1 revision criteria include concern about:

- persistent cross-model incompatibility;
- inability to distinguish epistemic from structural underdetermination;
- I repeatedly collapsing into unexplained O/V;
- inability to revise I under richer O/V reconstruction.

The current positive-I regression adds a complementary candidate failure:

> **I repeatedly collapsing into O/V even when positive selection evidence is independently established.**

This candidate failure should be tested further before any semantic revision.

---

# Recommended next step

Do not revise v4.1 immediately.

Do not modify v0.2 yet.

First perform a targeted experimental comparison between two candidate models:

## Model A — current v4.1

`I-function requires structural underdetermination + positive evidence`

## Model B — experimental split

`I-position = situated access`

`I-function = positively evidenced selective function`

`I-autonomy = residual structural underdetermination after O/V reconstruction`

Run the same positive-I cases through both models unchanged.

Evaluate:

- false-positive I assignment;
- false-negative I assignment;
- O/V contamination;
- explanatory gain;
- remove-and-compare behaviour;
- determinism sensitivity;
- cross-domain reproducibility.

# Provisional conclusion

The negative-I regressions showed that v0.2 successfully resists selectivity inflation.

The positive-I regressions now suggest the opposite risk:

> **selectivity suppression**

The current architecture appears very good at refusing unjustified I-functions.

It may be too strict to establish justified ones.

The central unresolved question is therefore no longer:

> “How do we stop everything from becoming I?”

but:

> **“Can I remain a genuine functional category even when its implementation is completely explained?”**

That question should be resolved before any canonical v4.2 revision is considered.
