# I·V·O I-POSITION PROTOCOL v0.3 — EXPERIMENTAL SEMANTICS

Status: EXPERIMENTAL / NON-CANONICAL / TEST-ONLY  
Canonical baseline: I·V·O Lens v4.1  
Supersedes: I-position protocol v0.2 for experimental testing only  
Does not modify: canonical I·V·O Lens v4.1  
Purpose: Test whether I-position, I-function, and I-autonomy should be treated as analytically distinct layers.

---

# 0. Governance

This protocol is an experimental execution layer.

It does not revise, supersede, reinterpret, or replace the frozen canonical I·V·O Lens v4.1.

Canonical O and V semantics remain unchanged.

Where this protocol conflicts with canonical v4.1, v4.1 remains authoritative.

Results produced with this protocol must be labelled experimental.

No result from v0.3 may be presented as evidence that canonical I has already been redefined.

---

# 1. Experimental hypothesis

The protocol tests the following hypothesis:

> Positionality, functional selection, and residual structural underdetermination are analytically separable.

The experimental split is:

1. **I-position** — where access, distinction, visibility, relevance, exclusion, or appraisal differs.
2. **I-function** — whether there is positive evidence for a selective function associated with that position.
3. **I-autonomy** — whether, after the strongest adequate O/V reconstruction, multiple continuations remain structurally open at the relevant selection point.

The protocol specifically tests whether this split:

- reduces false-negative I-function assignments;
- preserves the safeguards against unexplained = I;
- avoids selectivity inflation;
- preserves O/V explanatory integrity;
- prevents human privilege;
- prevents carrier proliferation;
- keeps autonomy claims strictly evidence-bound.

---

# 2. Canonical invariants

The following v4.1 commitments remain unchanged.

## O — Structured Possibility

O identifies structured possibility.

O does not mean:

- everything imaginable;
- uncertainty caused only by missing knowledge;
- an unspecified space of possibilities;
- retrospective alternatives that were never structurally available.

## V — Instantiated Relation

V identifies instantiated relations.

V includes realised:

- couplings;
- correlations;
- evaluations;
- rankings;
- registrations;
- interactions;
- selections;
- executions;

where these are observed or otherwise adequately supported.

Selection may therefore be represented in V as an instantiated event or relation.

v0.3 does not remove selection from V.

## Core epistemic safeguard

> unexplained ≠ I

Lack of explanation never establishes:

- I-position;
- I-function;
- I-autonomy.

## Structural openness safeguard

Epistemic underdetermination is not structural underdetermination.

Missing mechanism, missing measurement, missing model detail, or unresolved theory do not establish I-autonomy.

---

# 3. I-position

## 3.1 Definition

An **I-position** is a situated analytical position from which access, distinction, visibility, relevance, exclusion, registration, or appraisal differs materially within the chosen system boundary.

An I-position does not require:

- consciousness;
- agency;
- intention;
- subjectivity;
- free will;
- biological embodiment;
- human observation.

An I-position is not established merely because an entity exists in the system.

---

## 3.2 Position sufficiency rule

A candidate position is retained only when removing it causes loss of analytically relevant information that cannot be represented equally well by the remaining O/V structure.

Relevant loss may concern:

- what possibilities are accessible;
- what relations are visible;
- what distinctions are available;
- what is excluded;
- what is registered;
- what is relevant from that position;
- what remains outside view.

---

## 3.3 Remove-and-compare test

For every candidate I-position ask:

> If this position is removed, what analytically relevant information disappears?

Retain the position only if the answer identifies a non-trivial loss.

Do not retain a candidate merely because:

- it is physically coupled;
- it stores information;
- it transmits information;
- it occupies a location;
- it is a person;
- it is a subsystem;
- it is named as an observer.

---

## 3.4 Position anti-proliferation rule

Do not multiply positions when a smaller set preserves the same analytical distinctions.

If two candidate positions have no materially different:

- access;
- visibility;
- exclusion;
- relevance;
- appraisal;
- registration;

merge them or retain only the smaller analytically sufficient carrier.

---

## 3.5 Position status

Allowed statuses:

- `POSITION ESTABLISHED`
- `POSITION SUPPORTED`
- `POSITION UNRESOLVED`
- `POSITION NOT REQUIRED`

Use `ESTABLISHED` only when the positional difference itself is directly supplied or strongly evidenced.

Use `SUPPORTED` where the position is a reasonable reconstruction but not directly given.

---

# 4. Access discipline

Every retained I-position must distinguish evidence status for access claims.

Allowed access labels:

- `SUPPLIED ACCESS`
- `SUPPORTED ACCESS`
- `HYPOTHESIZED ACCESS`
- `OUTSIDE VIEW`
- `UNRESOLVED`

Do not infer unsupported:

- motives;
- emotions;
- beliefs;
- awareness;
- needs;
- intentions;
- internal representations;
- preferences;

from observed behaviour alone.

A position does not license mind-reading.

---

# 5. I-function

## 5.1 Experimental definition

An **I-function** is a positively evidenced selective function associated with an I-position.

A candidate I-function concerns whether multiple available possibilities are functionally:

- distinguished;
- ordered;
- weighted;
- admitted;
- excluded;
- prioritised;
- selected;

in a way that contributes to which continuation is instantiated.

---

## 5.2 Positive-evidence requirement

An I-function must never be inferred merely from the final outcome.

Positive evidence should, where available, include one or more independent traces such as:

- pre-outcome ranking;
- explicit selection state;
- command register;
- admission/exclusion record;
- criterion-sensitive ordering;
- reversible intervention on selection parameters;
- changed continuation after targeted intervention;
- evidence that alternatives were available before selection;
- evidence that non-selected alternatives were actually excluded.

The strongest cases combine multiple independent traces.

---

## 5.3 Functional selection does not require irreducibility

Under v0.3 experimental semantics:

> complete mechanistic explanation does not automatically revoke a positively evidenced I-function.

A function may be fully implemented by O/V structure.

The question:

> Does the function exist?

is distinguished from:

> Is the function irreducible or structurally autonomous?

The latter belongs under I-autonomy.

---

## 5.4 Selection terminology is insufficient

The following do not establish I-function by themselves:

- the word “selection”;
- filtering;
- weighting;
- thresholding;
- sorting;
- ranking;
- information storage;
- correlation;
- coupling;
- classification;
- switching;
- comparison.

A function is retained only if the full evidence shows that alternatives are functionally discriminated in a way relevant to instantiated continuation.

---

## 5.5 Selection versus ordering

Ordering alone does not necessarily establish I-function.

A sorting process may produce a rank order while leaving all alternatives equally retained and without affecting continuation.

For I-function, ordering must contribute to:

- exclusion;
- admission;
- prioritisation;
- routing;
- commitment;
- continuation selection;

or another demonstrable selective consequence.

---

## 5.6 Random selection

Random outcome generation does not automatically establish I-function.

A random process may instantiate one alternative without:

- criteria;
- relevance;
- positional discrimination;
- selective function beyond stochastic transition.

Random selection must therefore be analysed separately.

The mere fact that one branch occurs is insufficient.

---

## 5.7 I-function status

Allowed statuses:

- `I-FUNCTION ESTABLISHED`
- `I-FUNCTION SUPPORTED`
- `I-FUNCTION UNRESOLVED`
- `I-FUNCTION NOT ESTABLISHED`

Use `ESTABLISHED` only when positive evidence for functional selection exists independently of the final outcome.

Use `SUPPORTED` when evidence is substantial but incomplete.

Use `UNRESOLVED` when relevant evidence is missing or conflicting.

Use `NOT ESTABLISHED` when the strongest reconstruction does not support a selective function.

---

# 6. Mechanistic implementation

Mechanistic implementation is not itself an I-status.

It is a separate reconstruction dimension.

Allowed statuses:

- `MECHANISTIC IMPLEMENTATION COMPLETE`
- `MECHANISTIC IMPLEMENTATION PARTIAL`
- `MECHANISTIC IMPLEMENTATION UNRESOLVED`

The implementation layer asks:

> How is the observed function realised in O/V terms?

This may include:

- physical coupling;
- computation;
- criteria;
- weights;
- thresholds;
- network interactions;
- procedural rules;
- memory;
- feedback;
- distributed coordination.

Mechanistic completeness must not be treated as evidence against I-function.

Mechanistic incompleteness must not be treated as evidence for I-autonomy.

---

# 7. I-autonomy

## 7.1 Definition

**I-autonomy** is the stronger claim that, after the strongest adequate O/V reconstruction, more than one continuation remains structurally open at the relevant selection point.

I-autonomy is therefore a claim about residual structural underdetermination.

It is not equivalent to:

- unpredictability;
- complexity;
- ignorance;
- randomness;
- human choice;
- consciousness;
- computational opacity;
- lack of measurements.

---

## 7.2 Strong O/V challenge

Before assigning any positive I-autonomy status, construct the strongest available O/V reconstruction.

Include all relevant:

- constraints;
- relations;
- mechanisms;
- criteria;
- weights;
- state variables;
- coupling;
- history;
- intervention evidence.

Then ask:

> Does this reconstruction still leave multiple continuations structurally open at the relevant selection point?

If no:

`I-AUTONOMY NOT ESTABLISHED`

If unknown because mechanism or data are incomplete:

`I-AUTONOMY UNRESOLVED`

Only assign positive autonomy when structural openness is supported independently of ignorance.

---

## 7.3 I-autonomy status

Allowed statuses:

- `I-AUTONOMY ESTABLISHED`
- `I-AUTONOMY SUPPORTED`
- `I-AUTONOMY UNRESOLVED`
- `I-AUTONOMY NOT ESTABLISHED`

`ESTABLISHED` should be rare.

A positive autonomy claim requires direct evidence that is not reducible to missing knowledge.

---

# 8. Relationship between O, V and I

v0.3 does not create three mutually exclusive ontological categories.

The layers answer different analytical questions.

## O

> What continuations are structurally possible?

## V

> What relations and transitions are instantiated?

## I-position

> From where does access, distinction, relevance, visibility, or exclusion differ?

## I-function

> Is there positive evidence of a selective function affecting continuation?

## Mechanistic implementation

> How is that function realised through O/V structure?

## I-autonomy

> Does structural openness remain after the strongest O/V reconstruction?

A single empirical process may therefore be represented simultaneously as:

- V at the implementation/event level;
- I-function at the functional level.

This is not duplication if the levels answer different questions.

---

# 9. Required analysis sequence

Every v0.3 analysis must use the following order.

## Step 0 — Scope

Specify:

- object;
- source base;
- direction;
- scale;
- system boundary;
- excluded context.

Do not assume an observer in advance.

---

## Step 1 — Observation levels

Separate:

- observation;
- event;
- class;
- mechanism.

Use evidence statuses.

---

## Step 2 — O reconstruction

Identify structured possibilities.

Do not infer structural openness from uncertainty.

---

## Step 3 — V reconstruction

Identify instantiated relations.

Keep:

- ranking;
- selection;
- registration;
- execution;

inside V where empirically instantiated.

Do not remove them merely to create room for I.

---

## Step 4 — I-position scan

Generate only plausible candidates.

For every candidate:

1. define carrier;
2. specify access;
3. specify outside view;
4. run remove-and-compare;
5. discard redundant candidates;
6. assign one final position status.

---

## Step 5 — I-function test

For every retained position ask:

1. Are multiple alternatives actually available?
2. Are they accessible or distinguishable from the position?
3. Is there positive evidence of ordering, admission, exclusion, prioritisation, or selection?
4. Is the selective step distinguishable from final execution?
5. Does intervention on the selective structure alter continuation?
6. Is the function inferred independently of the outcome alone?

Assign exactly one final I-function status.

---

## Step 6 — Mechanistic implementation

Construct the strongest O/V implementation model.

Assign:

- COMPLETE;
- PARTIAL;
- UNRESOLVED.

Do not use this step to automatically revoke I-function.

---

## Step 7 — I-autonomy test

Run the strongest O/V-only challenge.

Ask:

> After the strongest reconstruction, does more than one continuation remain structurally open?

Assign exactly one final autonomy status.

---

## Step 8 — Cross-position analysis

Where multiple I-positions exist, compare:

- accessible O;
- accessible V;
- selective function;
- excluded alternatives;
- blind spots;
- relevance structure.

Do not infer that:

- wider access = more correct;
- different meaning = different reality;
- stronger position = stronger autonomy.

---

## Step 9 — Outside view

Use the following categories:

### INACCESSIBLE
Relevant information exists but is not accessible from the position.

### UNOBSERVED
Potentially observable information is not present in the current evidence.

### UNRESOLVED
The evidence does not justify a positive or negative conclusion.

### UNDEFINED WITHIN MODEL
The relevant concept has no defined meaning inside the chosen model.

### OUTSIDE SYSTEM BOUNDARY
The information lies outside the declared scope.

Do not collapse:

- unresolved into inaccessible;
- inaccessible into nonexistent;
- undefined into unknown.

---

## Step 10 — Synthesis

Report separately:

1. O findings;
2. V findings;
3. I-position findings;
4. I-function findings;
5. mechanistic implementation;
6. I-autonomy;
7. smallest supported conclusion;
8. revocation conditions.

---

# 10. Mandatory revocation conditions

Every positive I-position and I-function assignment must include a revocation condition.

Examples:

## I-position revocation

Revoke if a richer reconstruction shows that the purported positional difference adds no information beyond existing V-relations.

## I-function revocation

Revoke if the apparent selective trace is shown to be:

- passive recording;
- post-hoc labelling;
- non-causal sorting;
- epiphenomenal ranking;
- a threshold transition with no distinct selective contribution;
- an artefact of measurement.

## I-autonomy revocation

Revoke if a stronger adequate O/V reconstruction closes the previously open continuations.

---

# 11. Carrier rules

A carrier is the smallest system unit in which the relevant positional or selective structure is realised.

Possible carriers may include:

- individual;
- subsystem;
- controller;
- cell;
- organism;
- team;
- committee-in-procedure;
- distributed technical system.

A carrier is not automatically:

- a conscious subject;
- an agent;
- an ontological entity.

The carrier is an analytical system boundary.

Use the smallest carrier that preserves the relevant function.

# 13. Failure checks

Every test should report PASS / FAIL / UNRESOLVED for the following.

## FC-1 Selectivity inflation

Does the protocol incorrectly classify mere filtering, coupling, ranking, or thresholding as I-function?

## FC-2 Unknown → autonomy leakage

Does missing mechanistic knowledge become evidence of I-autonomy?

## FC-3 Human privilege

Are humans granted I-function or autonomy on the basis of being human?

## FC-4 Position proliferation

Are redundant positions created without analytical gain?

## FC-5 Group-mind inflation

Are collectives treated as unitary subjects without need?

## FC-6 Procedure reification

Is a rule or procedure treated as an actor rather than structure?

## FC-7 O/V erasure

Are observed relations removed from V in order to create I?

## FC-8 Function suppression

Is positively evidenced selection deleted solely because it is mechanistically explainable?

## FC-9 Randomness inflation

Is stochastic branching mistaken for functional selection?

## FC-10 Recording inflation

Is passive registration mistaken for selective function?

## FC-11 Sorting inflation

Is ordering without selective consequence mistaken for I-function?

## FC-12 Carrier drift

Does the carrier change arbitrarily across equivalent reconstructions?

# 15. Near-miss adversarial suite

Before any canonical consideration, v0.3 must be tested against cases designed to trigger false-positive I.

Minimum suite:

## NM-01 Passive filter

A filter passes inputs meeting a fixed physical condition and blocks others.

Question:

Does v0.3 incorrectly establish I-function when the behaviour is fully passive?

---

## NM-02 Thermostat

A thermostat switches heating on below a threshold and off above it.

Question:

Does deterministic thresholding alone trigger I-function?

---

## NM-03 Lookup table

Each input maps one-to-one to one fixed output.

Question:

Does formal mapping become “selection” merely because multiple table entries exist?

---

## NM-04 Random number generator

One of several alternatives is randomly instantiated.

Question:

Does stochastic branching get confused with selective function?

---

## NM-05 Physical bifurcation

A physical system enters one branch due to boundary conditions or instability without a distinct selective carrier.

Question:

Does branching itself produce I?

---

## NM-06 Sorting algorithm

The system orders alternatives but retains all of them.

Question:

Can v0.3 distinguish ordering from selection?

---

## NM-07 Passive information register

A register records which event occurred but does not influence continuation.

Question:

Does recording become I-function?

---

## NM-08 Epiphenomenal committee

A committee evaluates and votes, but an external rule always determines the final project regardless of the committee result.

Question:

Does apparent selection remain I-function when intervention shows it has no influence on continuation?

---

# 16. Positive regression suite

After the near-miss suite, rerun unchanged:

- AB-01 deterministic algorithm;
- AB-02 energy controller;
- AB-03 controlled human choice;
- AB-04 committee-in-procedure.

Expected test question:

> Does v0.3 preserve positive selection recognition while still rejecting near-miss false positives?

No expected status should be hard-coded into the prompt.

# 17. Success criteria for v0.3

v0.3 passes the experimental phase only if all of the following hold.

## SC-1

Near-miss false-positive rate remains acceptably low.

## SC-2

Positive-I regressions no longer collapse automatically into O/V when selection is independently evidenced.

## SC-3

I-autonomy remains rare and evidence-bound.

## SC-4

Incomplete mechanism never becomes positive autonomy evidence.

## SC-5

O and V remain intact.

## SC-6

Carrier assignments remain minimal and reproducible.

## SC-7

The same cases produce substantially similar classifications across repeated runs and, ideally, across multiple model families.

## SC-8

The protocol adds discriminative information rather than merely new terminology.

---

# 18. Failure criteria

v0.3 should be rejected or revised if any of the following recur systematically.

## RF-1

Thermostats, passive filters, registers, or lookup tables routinely receive I-function without additional evidence.

## RF-2

Randomness is repeatedly interpreted as selection.

## RF-3

Human or organisational cases receive autonomy merely because mechanism is incomplete.

## RF-4

I-position proliferates across physically coupled subsystems.

## RF-5

O/V structure is weakened to preserve I.

## RF-6

Carrier choice becomes unstable or arbitrary.

## RF-7

The distinction between I-function and I-autonomy cannot be reproduced across models.

# 19. Experimental interpretation guide

The following combinations are allowed.

## Case A

`POSITION ESTABLISHED`  
`I-FUNCTION ESTABLISHED`  
`MECHANISTIC IMPLEMENTATION COMPLETE`  
`I-AUTONOMY NOT ESTABLISHED`

Meaning:

A selective function is evidenced and fully mechanistically reconstructed. No residual structural autonomy is established.

---

## Case B

`POSITION ESTABLISHED`  
`I-FUNCTION ESTABLISHED`  
`MECHANISTIC IMPLEMENTATION PARTIAL`  
`I-AUTONOMY UNRESOLVED`

Meaning:

Selection is evidenced, but implementation is incomplete and autonomy remains undecided.

---

## Case C

`POSITION ESTABLISHED`  
`I-FUNCTION NOT ESTABLISHED`  
`MECHANISTIC IMPLEMENTATION COMPLETE`  
`I-AUTONOMY NOT ESTABLISHED`

Meaning:

A relevant position exists, but no selective function is independently evidenced.

---

## Case D

`POSITION NOT REQUIRED`  
`I-FUNCTION NOT ESTABLISHED`  
`I-AUTONOMY NOT ESTABLISHED`

Meaning:

The analysis is adequately reconstructed in O/V without a material position or selective function.

---

## Case E

`POSITION ESTABLISHED`  
`I-FUNCTION ESTABLISHED`  
`I-AUTONOMY SUPPORTED/ESTABLISHED`

Meaning:

This is a strong claim.

It requires evidence that structural openness remains after the strongest adequate O/V reconstruction.

This outcome should be rare and heavily audited.

# 20. Canonical decision gate

No canonical revision should be considered until:

1. the adversarial near-miss suite is complete;
2. the positive regression suite is rerun unchanged;
3. cross-model reproducibility is tested;
4. failure modes are documented;
5. a semantic comparison between v4.1 and v0.3 is frozen;
6. an explicit governance decision is made.

The decision question after testing is:

> Does the position / function / autonomy split produce greater explanatory discrimination with fewer false positives and fewer false negatives than canonical v4.1, while preserving O/V integrity and epistemic safeguards?

Only if the answer is supported should a future canonical revision be considered.

# 21. Current status

v0.3 is not a proposed canonical replacement.

It is an experimental semantic hypothesis generated from the following evidence:

- v0.2 position regressions;
- positive-I regression failure;
- AB-01 deterministic algorithm;
- AB-02 autonomous energy controller;
- AB-03 controlled human choice;
- AB-04 committee-in-procedure.

The purpose of v0.3 is now to attempt to falsify the three-way split.

A successful experimental outcome is not:

> “v0.3 finds more I.”

A successful outcome is:

> **v0.3 distinguishes position, function, implementation, and autonomy more accurately than the current combined criterion, while resisting false-positive I assignments.**
